Internal (process): a lead measure, a scoreboard, and an accountability shape — the parts of 4DX that transfer to an agent loop.

**The gap.** `epic-pm` disciplines scope *at the gate*. Nothing told you mid-flight whether an epic was winning: between the objective gate and the wrap — weeks — the only signal was the coordinator's status table, and every column in it is activity. An epic could look healthy the whole way and land on a Proof that failed. The distinction that closes it is lead vs lag, which we were already applying correctly in exactly one place: `distill-lessons` on the development loop, where *"rounds-to-approval trending down is the proof the harness is improving"* is textbook lead-measure reasoning. It had never been applied to the product outcome.

**Five edits, no new surface.** `docs/objectives.md` gains a finish line in *X to Y by when* form with a lead measure of its own (two blanks — the date and the coverage count — are left visible rather than guessed, since a fabricated finish line reads as agreed). `orchestration.md` gains a cap of two concurrent epics, and states the part that gets misread: **the cap is on objectives, not work items** — eight issues under one epic is throughput and is untouched, four epics under four objectives is the violation. `epic-pm`'s objective grows from four lines to five, the new one being a **lead measure** that inherits the Proof line's constraint verbatim (readable off work the epic already produces, never an instrument we'd have to build) and defaults to the count of issues whose real-model goal check passes — deliberately not the count merged, because merging is activity and a passing goal check is evidence. `epic-spec-template.md` §4 becomes *Scoreboard & running index*: three lines answering *are we winning* above the audit log that answers *what happened*, with a per-issue Goal check column the Lead line is counted off. `epic-em`'s report becomes **Account → Review → Plan**, and `plan-day` gains a daily Account plus a ⭑ mark for tasks serving the objective.

**Nothing on the scoreboard is hand-maintained**, because a number a human must remember to update is one that will be wrong, and a stale scoreboard is worse than none — it reads as evidence. The Lead line is counted off the Goal check column rather than asserted beside it, and the `epic-agent` refreshes both from the PR handles it already reads at fold time. That cadence is the honest limit: a fold-less stretch leaves a scoreboard as old as the last fold. Making it wake-fresh means carrying a `goalCheck` field through the status table and `epic-wake`'s args, which is a real change to a script with a verification harness and was deliberately not bundled into a documentation change.

**What was left out, and why.** No WIG session (no team to convene; event-driven wakes beat a weekly meeting for agents — D4's shape was taken, its ceremony wasn't). No cascade of objectives down to sub-issues (that's the per-issue goal check with a second vocabulary over it). No vocabulary rename: **lead measure** is adopted because we had no word for it, **WIG** is not, since "objective" is already load-bearing across four documents. The underlying reason so much of 4DX doesn't transfer is that it is built against the *whirlwind* — urgent day-job work crowding out the strategic goal — and agents have no whirlwind. Our failure mode is the inverse: plenty of motion, no discrimination. That predicts which future borrowings will be useful here and which will be cargo.

`docs/internal/4dx-process.md` is the narrative and carries the ownership map, so each rule stays stated once (tenet 5; `stale-restatement` is a live class in the cycle-ledger). It also gives the process its own kill line — a stale scoreboard, a lead measure that only ever rises, or an Account that is never wrong all mean the instrument has gone decorative.
