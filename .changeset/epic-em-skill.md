Internal (skills): add `epic-em` (FIX-1073) — a standing engineering-manager posture over `epic-lifecycle` for running an epic without bottlenecking on the user's approval. It composes `epic-lifecycle` and `docs/contributing/asking-for-decisions.md` rather than restating them, adds no state and no step, and **moves no gate**: the epic-objective gate, every per-issue spec approval, and every merge stay the user's, because an absorbed spec gate only relocates unreviewed direction to the merge gate (the worst moment to change one) and an absorbed merge gate puts unreviewed code on `main`. What changes is the traffic between the gates — an escalated *engineering* blocker skips step 4's surfacing and is decided and recorded rather than handed up, with only a critical product or architectural call going to the user, and a wake leads with outcomes rather than a phase table. One rule bounds all of it: **absorbing the judgment does not shorten the path to it.** The EM decides from what a worker reported and dispatches a bounded reader when that isn't enough, never opening a spec, diff or review thread in the coordinator (`orchestration.md` → "The coordinator dispatches; it never does the work") — which the PR-feedback cap forces, since the wake's cap blocker puts the converging-vs-looping analysis on the PR itself. Answers are recorded where `epic-lifecycle` already puts them: `blockerResolutions` for a row-keyed blocker, `epic.answers` plus an `epic-agent` fold for an epic-keyed one, since using the row field at epic scope loses the answer and blocks `mayWrap`. Non-absorbable by construction: `INCONCLUSIVE` POC verdicts, epic-level `unsettled` claims, and epic-spec open questions. At the cap the product-critical test is applied to each resolution as it arises rather than pre-classifying the five options — "merge as-is with a known gap" always goes up, and "split the remainder into a follow-up" goes up whenever it defers promised or customer-visible behaviour. Registered in `CLAUDE.md` → Workflow skills with a one-line back-link from `epic-lifecycle`'s Boundaries; `docs/contributing/orchestration.md` is deliberately untouched, since `epic-em` is a posture and not a third lifecycle.
