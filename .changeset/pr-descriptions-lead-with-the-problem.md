Internal (skills/process): PR descriptions now lead with the problem, and the reviewer contract moves below the fold.

**The defect.** A spec PR opened with ~40 lines of reviewer contract — text identical on every spec PR — then asked the reviewer to look closely at three numbered Decisions, and only then stated what was broken. The first screen was boilerplate, the ask came before the context needed to answer it, and the problem statement was roughly sixty lines down. A description written that way gets skimmed, and a skimmed description means the direction review — the only review a human can give and a bot can't — doesn't happen.

**The fix is ordering, not less content.** A new `docs/contributing/writing-for-humans.md` carries the general rule (**the fold**): above it, only what a human needs to act, problem first, with nothing preceding the problem — not a ref, not a label, not a statement of what kind of artifact this is. Below it, everything else collapsed rather than deleted. It also carries per-artifact word budgets (~150 above the fold for an implementation PR, ~400 for a spec PR, ~100 for a comment), the two lists that keep the fold honest (**what never goes above** — derivations, enumerations, verification output, rejected alternatives; **what never gets collapsed** — a decision being asked of you, a risk, a known gap, anything hard to reverse, scope that grew), and five density checks for the sentence-level half of the same job. Collapsing costs the other reader nothing: an automated reviewer fetches raw markdown, where `<details>` is just text.

`pr-reviewer-guidance.md` applies it to PRs and is canonical for the layout — problem · what this does · what's asked of you · parts worth reviewing closely · links line, then the contract and the long-form case collapsed. It gains **diagram guidance**: one mermaid diagram when the change is a race (`sequenceDiagram`), a path through layers (`flowchart LR`), a dependency shape (`flowchart TD`) or a state machine, with before/after named as the highest-value shape, a ~10-node ceiling, and the rule that a diagram restating the prose beside it means one of them goes. A before/after worked example of a real spec PR shows the whole reorder.

Two ordering rules do the real work. **The ask comes after the context** — pointing a reviewer at "Decision 4" before they know what problem Decision 4 serves is asking them to review a number, which is why *"Parts worth reviewing closely"* moved below the problem and the solution rather than above them. And **bad news never collapses** — a known gap found by a reviewer is reported as an oversight and costs a round explaining it was on purpose.

Propagated to the surfaces that author descriptions: `issue-spec` Step 6 and `issue-implement` Step 9 now specify the five above-the-fold blocks and the word budget instead of "the two reviewer blocks"; `epic-agent` and `issue-worker` follow at their altitudes; both spec templates rename their contract to `How to review this` and say it is pasted into a collapsed block. BP-039 widens from "specs lead with a plain-language summary" to the fold across every artifact a human reads. One constraint worth stating: `<details>` belongs in a PR body and not in `docs/specs/<ISSUE-ID>.md`, which is mirrored verbatim to a Linear document that renders the tag as raw HTML — the spec's own Part I / Part II split is already its fold.
