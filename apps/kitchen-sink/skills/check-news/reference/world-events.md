# World Events

Extra guidance when the question is about geopolitics, elections, armed conflict, natural disasters, protests, legal proceedings, or any developing event with a human cost. Load this on top of the core `check-news` playbook.

## Recency targets

- **Breaking events** (first 72 hours) → accept only sources from the past 24–48 hours.
- **Developing stories** → last 7 days, with a re-check for the latest update.
- **Aftermath / analysis** → last 30 days, acknowledging earlier framing may have shifted.

Many "current" world-events pages on the web are stale — a tragedy from 2022 can out-rank yesterday's update for SEO reasons. Always check the date.

## High-signal sources

Prefer primary and wire-service reporting:

1. **Wire services**: Reuters, Associated Press, Agence France-Presse, Bloomberg. Factual, dateline-stamped, globally distributed.
2. **Public-service broadcasters**: BBC, NPR, CBC, ABC (Australia), Deutsche Welle, France 24, NHK.
3. **Major newspapers of record**: NYT, WaPo, Guardian, FT, Le Monde, El País. Check paywall access before citing.
4. **Official statements**: government releases, UN/ICRC/WHO dispatches, court filings. Primary beats reporting of primary.
5. **Local reporting** when the event is local. A local outlet often has details the wires lack.

Lower-signal:

- **Partisan outlets on either side** — useful for the framing but weigh against balanced sources.
- **Tweets from officials** — useful as a pointer to the official statement; don't cite the tweet if the statement is linked.

Avoid:

- Rumor aggregators, Telegram-style channels without sourcing, and machine-translated claim chains.
- "AI news" summaries of geopolitical events — they often smooth over contested details.

## Search query patterns

- `<event> latest` — add a date-scoping word.
- `<location> news <month> <year>`
- `site:reuters.com <topic>` or `site:apnews.com <topic>`
- `<person or entity> statement <topic>`

Cross-reference across at least two wire services. If only one reports a claim, say so.

## Handling uncertainty

World-events questions have more uncertainty than most. Standard practice:

- **Distinguish confirmed vs reported vs claimed**: "Reuters confirms", "AFP reports per local officials", "social media claims unverified".
- **Casualty and damage figures change**: almost always. Use the most recent estimate from a named authoritative source (e.g. "as of 2026-04-22, per UN OCHA: X displaced").
- **Motivation and cause are often contested**: separate the event from the explanation. "A bombing occurred" is fact; "caused by Y" is often early reporting.
- **Misinformation is routine**: false flag claims, staged videos, AI-generated imagery. If a viral claim is central to the question, note that verification is still in progress.

## Common traps

- **Dated headline recycling**: an old tragedy resurfaces on social. Check the date.
- **Translation drift**: a statement in one language takes on extra intensity in English. Quote the original if possible.
- **Map / place confusion**: two places with the same name. Be explicit about country and region.
- **Timeline compression**: events spread over weeks get described as if they happened at once. Keep dates attached.

## When to caveat

Open the answer with a caveat when:

- The freshest primary source is older than the target window.
- Casualty or damage figures are the main question and reports diverge.
- A claim is widely circulating but not yet confirmed by a wire service.
- Machine translation or AI summary is involved — flag what's first-hand and what's derivative.
