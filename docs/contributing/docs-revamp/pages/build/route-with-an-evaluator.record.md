## 2 · Cold-read questions

Written by an agent that had seen no draft. Pass 9 got these unchanged.

1. On the support desk, the `classify-ticket` evaluator asks a `team` choice question with options `billing` and `technical`. A customer writes "I was charged twice this month." What does the evaluator hand back to your code, how do you read the team, and does the evaluator itself send the ticket anywhere?
   Answer: It returns `{ answers }`, keyed by question id, so the team is `answers.team.choice`, typed as `"billing" | "technical"`, not `string` (evt:76-129, blk:353). A choice answer is `{ type: "choice", choice, probabilities?, confidence? }`, and `confidence` is there only when the model reported one. The evaluator never supplies it (evt:76-88, blk:377). The evaluator makes one call, answers and stops. It doesn't retry, doesn't fall back to another model and doesn't decide anything with the answer. Branching is your code's job: a `router` or a sequencer step (ev:303-306, blk:383). Nothing goes to the client. What was asked and answered shows in the DevTool trace (blk:383).
2. The desk's tree has a `department` level (billing / technical) and, under billing only, a `billing-urgency` level (urgent / routine), with leaves `urgentBilling`, `billingQueue`, `techQueue` and `review` as `ambiguous`. Floors are 0.6 on billing, technical and urgent, and 0.5 on routine. Jev answers `billing` with confidence 0.8, then `urgent` with 0.65. Which blocks run, what does the urgency evaluator look at, and what does the router return?
   Answer: The billing edge opens (0.8 >= 0.6), so the tree goes to `root/billing`. The urgency edge opens (0.65 >= 0.6), so `urgentBilling` runs (ex/src/route.ts:25-68; gate:42-60). The urgency evaluator is asked about the ticket itself, not about level 1's answer. Every evaluator and leaf receives the router's input (cr:81-85, ert:139-150). The router returns whatever the chosen leaf returns, here `urgentBilling`'s `{ queue }` (cr:146-155). Levels on other branches never run and cost nothing. A ticket routed to `technical` would never call the urgency model (util:1061, ert:167-175). The trace shows a verdict for `root` and `root/billing` (ert:183-195).
3. Jev answers `billing` with confidence exactly 0.6 on one ticket, and 0.59 on the next. Where does each ticket go? On the one that goes to `review`, does `review` know why it got the ticket? *(edge case)*
   Answer: Floors are compared inclusively, so 0.6 opens the billing edge and the tree goes on to the urgency level. At 0.59 the edge doesn't open, and the ticket goes to `review` with reason `below-floor` (gate:58; crt:41-47, crt:51-55). `review` receives only the router's input, the ticket. It gets neither the reason nor the model's answer. Those live in the trace verdict `{ level, on, ambiguous: "below-floor", choice }` (cr:136-141, cr:228-229, cr:281-289). There are three reasons in all: `no-confidence`, `below-floor` and `no-branch` (gate:15).
4. You swap the evaluation model from `typesafe-ai/jev` to `openai.evaluationModel("gpt-6-luna")`. The model clearly picks `billing` for an obvious billing ticket, and its `probabilities` put billing at 0.97. Where do your tickets go now, and what would you change if you want to branch on that model's bare answer? *(edge case)*
   Answer: Every ticket goes to `review` with reason `no-confidence`, whatever the model chose, and level 2 is never asked. OpenAI's evaluation model reports no confidence (cr:225-226, util:1045, ert:183-195; goal: three of three tickets went to review). A choice's `probabilities` are never read as confidence, however high (gate:9-11, crt:104-122). No confidence means the key is absent, not that confidence is low, and the gate won't fill it in (crt:57-67). To branch on the bare answer, put a plain `router` after the evaluator that reads `answers.team.choice`. That router doesn't route on confidence, and its leaves receive the evaluator's answers, not the ticket (util:1045-1057, blk:584).
5. Mid-shift the model provider goes down and the evaluation call fails with `provider down: 503`. Does the ticket end up in `review`, the doubtful pile? How do you keep the action from failing? *(edge case)*
   Answer: No. A failed evaluation, such as a provider error or a refused model, fails the router with that error and does not go to `ambiguous`. In the test, `review` never received the ticket (cr:9-10, cr:226; ert:307-314). To catch it, wrap the router in a sequencer and add `.rescue`, which catches that error (ert:315-323, util:1059). (A request cancelled mid-level ends cancelled, not failed and not ambiguous: ert:325-348.)
6. You want a person to look at every doubtful ticket before it's routed, not just have it land in a queue called "review". What do you have to write, what comes back, and what does the router need from the host? A second support agent then tries to answer a customer's paused ticket from their own account. Can they? *(edge case)*
   Answer: The router doesn't hand anything to a person on its own. It runs the one `ambiguous` block you supply, and the example's `review` just returns `{ queue: "review" }` (cr:136-141; ex/src/route.ts:8-9, 19-23). To ask a person, the `ambiguous` block calls `ctx.suspendForUser({ kind: "review", prompt })` (kinds are `approval`, `review` and `input`). The request stops until the user answers. A review returns `{ cancelled: false, answer }`, or `{ cancelled: true }` if the task is cancelled while it waits (sfu:1-26, sfu:75-76). This needs durable execution. That's on wherever stores persist, and `fsdev run` and `fsdev dev` get it over local SQLite (sfu:19, dx:28). On resume the evaluators aren't asked again (ert:366-398). Caveat: suspending inside a leaf is tested, but suspending in `ambiguous` itself isn't [verify]. The second agent can't answer it. A paused request answers only its own user, "same user, same tenant, and ... the same organization". Another user's request is answered as a missing one (route-utils.ts:186-211, request-principal.ts:41-52; the route guard wasn't read).
