## 2 · Cold-read questions

Written by an agent that had seen no draft. Pass 9 got these unchanged.

1. Alice opens the app and edits engineering.lead's instructions. Bob then sends the lead a message. What does Bob's turn run, what does each of their rosters show, and what happens to the WORKER.md file? *(edge case)*
   Answer: Bob's turn runs the file's version, and his roster row shows the lead unedited (edited: false). The file is never written. Alice's edit copied the lead onto her own roster under the same id, engineering.lead, and applied the change to that copy. Her roster lists the lead once, still standard, marked edited: true. Her conversations, tasks and messages stay where they are, and her next turn runs the copy. A reset deletes her copy, so her next turn reads the file again. Facts: section 6 'Edit' and 'Reset' (wk/workers/hire-blocks.ts:11-14, :81-90, :213-247, :262-277; wt/worker-roster.test.ts:286-310, :371-383); section 12 question 1.
2. Alice asks engineering.lead to write some code. Its file lists delegates: [engineering.coder, engineering.reviewer] and tells it not to write code itself. What happens, and how does Alice's conversation find out how it went?
   Answer: The lead files a task on its conversation's board for engineering.coder, one of its delegates that takes tasks (the coder runs on the built-in agent flow, which takes tasks with no setup). The coder works the task as one turn in a task session of its own, with its own instructions, tools and model. Its message is the task's title, goal and context, not the goal alone. Its answer is stored as the task's result, and the task completes. When the task ends, the lead's conversation is told once, in a line under the coder's name. The lead does not write the code itself. Facts: section 5 (dw:10-14, ov:130, bw:114, co:464-472, wt/any-worker-files.test.ts:167-188); flag 4; section 12 question 2. (The sheet says the page may state that the lead is told when the task ends, but not what it does next.)
3. Your support team needs a worker that follows fixed steps and gets a review before anything is sent. A teammate who doesn't write TypeScript keeps its instructions up to date. What do you change, and what stays a document they can still edit?
   Answer: You write a flow in the app's code that holds the steps and the review, register it as a worker flow, and give its name in the worker's flow: setting (for example flow: request-triage in workforce/teams/support/workers/triage/WORKER.md). A WORKER.md is data: behaviour lives in the flow it names. The file stays a document with a description and instructions, and a non-TypeScript author can still edit it. The app talks to the worker the same way as before, through respond. If the worker should still take tasks from other workers, the new flow has to declare that it takes tasks (the contributor role). Facts: section 7 (wd:759, wd:827, wd:37, wd:802-813, wd:408-411, wd:454-477); flag 5; section 12 question 3.
4. You're building a page that shows Alice the tasks her lead has handed out. Where do those tasks live, and how does the page read them?
   Answer: On the board of the conversation that filed them. Every session of a worker that files keeps its own board, and a worker files onto the board of the session it is in. A browser reads a user's boards through the tasks collection on any of that user's sessions, on a flow that keeps boards (the built-in worker flow, for example). The read covers every session that user has filed from, and never another user's. Ready-made components are BoardColumns and BoardList (and Roster) from @flow-state-dev/workforce/react. A task's input, output and metadata are never sent to the browser. Facts: section 5 'Where tasks are shown to a page' (dw:169, dw:188-190, ui:60-130); section 12 question 4.
5. Bob wants to use Alice's engineering.lead: open its conversation with her, read what it remembers about her, or name it as a delegate of a worker he hired. What does he get? *(edge case)*
   Answer: Nothing of Alice's. Alice's lead answers to Alice. Bob cannot open her sessions, name her lead as a delegate, or read what it remembers about her. Another user's worker gets the same answer as a worker that doesn't exist. Bob has a lead on his own roster (the standard worker from the same file), but it is a separate worker: his sessions and what it learns about him stay with him, and workers on agent never share what they keep between users. Facts: section 6 (ov:26, dh:99-104, wt/worker-roster.test.ts:57-67); section 4 (wd:875-877); 'What a built-in worker keeps' (wd:277-279).
6. Someone commits a WORKER.md with broken frontmatter (or one with no description) and also leaves a stray file in the workforce/ folder, then deploys. What happens when the app starts? *(edge case)*
   Answer: The app doesn't start. The Workforce kit reads the folder at startup, and every file in it is either used or refused. A file workforce doesn't read stops the app starting, and the error names the file. A WORKER.md that doesn't load, such as one with broken frontmatter or no description (the only key a file must have), stops the start too, and the error names its folder. The refusal rejects the app's ready(). Facts: section 3 (kp:285-289, wk/loader/read-workforce-folder.ts:1-12, :84-91, wt/workforce-kit.test.ts:77-107); section 2 (wd:76, wk/loader/read-workforce-directory.ts:275-278).
