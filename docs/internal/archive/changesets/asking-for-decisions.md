Internal (skills/process): asks put to the user are now framed as business decisions, with a recommendation.

**The defect.** Nothing owned what an *ask* contains. `writing-for-humans.md` owns ordering, `pr-reviewer-guidance.md` owns PR layout, `orchestration.md` owns the gates — but the content of a decision put to a human was left to each surface, and each one defaulted to the engineer's register. A spec's §6 listed `decision / rejected / locks in`. A gate surfaced as a PR link. `issue-spec` explicitly instructed "present options with trade-offs and let the project owner decide" — a neutral fork, from the one party who had done the research. A worker's escalated blocker travelled to the coordinator as a phrase, and the coordinator (which never read the code) had nothing to build an ask from.

**The fix.** A new `docs/contributing/asking-for-decisions.md` carries the role contract and the shape. You are the engineer; the user is the product owner — they set the objectives and track the process as a whole, and their being technical is a trap rather than a licence, because every paragraph spent re-deriving a settled call is attention not spent on the objective only they are tracking. Every ask is translated into the decision they are actually making, priced in customers, promises, timing and reversibility. Six parts: **the fork as the heading** (a plain-language either/or) · **plain terms** (observable behaviour, no paths or symbols; test — could they explain it back to a customer?) · **the trade-off** (in their currency, naming who outside the room feels it) · **my recommendation** (always) · **what would change my mind** (the fact you don't have and they might — the part that goes missing, and the one that makes the question theirs to answer) · **what being wrong costs** (calibration, so a cheap call doesn't steal attention from an expensive one). Dropping the user into the engineer's chair stays possible and is now explicitly rare: say out loud that it's happening and why no business framing decides it.

**Two rules keep it from inflating.** Most decisions are being *ratified*, not made — a spec's §6 rows stay one line each with a cost column, and only the one or two genuine live forks (plus every §12 open question) get the full shape. And several cases are not asks at all: the call is the implementer's, the answer is derivable, or it's a coin flip with near-zero cost either way — decide it and note it in a line. Under-asking hides a call that shapes the product; over-asking trains the reader to skim the one that mattered.

Wired through the surfaces that already talk to the user: tenet 6 in `philosophy.md` (altitude is also *whose chair they sit in*), BP-041, the spec and epic-spec templates, the PR-description layout (which now separates ratified rows from live forks), both lifecycles' gate-surfacing, and the challenger. Worker escalations changed shape too — `issue-worker`, `epic-agent` and `cross-spec-review` now return the ask's parts rather than a topic, since the coordinator holds status lines and cannot reconstruct a decision from a phrase.
