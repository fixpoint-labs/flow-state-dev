Internal (skills/process): a PR's *What's asked of you* now carries only the decisions the product owner actually owns.

**The defect.** The layout was right and the register was wrong. A spec PR reached the user with a six-row decision table in which one row was the mechanism the section above had already described, one was a pure implementation call ("feed the new lenses from the shared evidence bundle rather than wiring data to each one"), and one was an obviously-correct choice that cost nothing to reverse — all typeset identically to the two rows that genuinely changed what a reader of our reports would see. Below it sat a ~450-word live fork and three dense paragraphs of engineering prose, with no marker anywhere saying where the product owner's job ended. The result is a reader who finishes six rows unable to name which one was theirs, which is a more expensive failure than a long description: it converts a decision into a rubber stamp.

Two rules were feeding it. `pr-reviewer-guidance.md` §3 policed the *cost* column ("write it in consequences, not mechanism") but never the Decision column itself, so an implementation call with a well-written cost cell passed. And "where the artifact defines a sign-off surface, the cap doesn't apply — show all of it" was read as licence to index all eight of a spec's §6 rows onto the surface a product owner reads, even though `writing-for-humans.md` already carried the sharper test — is the reader being asked to *decide* on this row, or merely *informed* of it.

**The gate — two filters, in `asking-for-decisions.md`.** It already owned what an ask contains and when not to ask, and these govern every ask surface (conversation, gate, spec, PR), so they live there and everywhere else links. **Is it theirs?** A wrong answer has to change one of four things: what a user experiences, what we've promised anyone outside the team, when something ships, or what it costs to undo. **Is it worth their attention?** A call that is obviously right or reversible at near-zero cost is one you make and note, because surfacing it *as* a decision implies weight it doesn't have. **Three is the ceiling, live forks included** — the same number now in `spec-template.md` §6, which previously allowed eight.

**Filtering is sorting, not hiding.** Everything the filters drop lands in a new collapsed **engineering calls** block, one bullet each, in the engineer's register. That is what makes the gate safe on a spec PR where §6 is what approval certifies: nothing leaves the record and nothing is approved unseen.

**The shape — no table.** A grid makes the reader parse cells and compare columns before they can read one decision, and it sizes the call and its cost to fit a column rather than to be understood. Each decision is a subheading stating it in plain language, with two bullets under it. Since a live fork's heading already worked that way, ratified decisions and forks now read as one list of headings a reader can answer from.

**The audience boundary.** The layout table gains a *For* column, and block 4 (*Parts worth reviewing closely*) opens by saying the audience has changed to the code reviewer — so a product owner stops at the end of block 3 rather than skimming mechanism looking for another ask.

`pr-reviewer-guidance.md` §3 keeps what is genuinely PR-specific: the rule that a row restating what the change does belongs to block 2, the shape, and the worked before/after that runs a real six-row table through both filters and lands on one fork and two decisions. `issue-spec`, `issue-implement` and `epic-agent` carry a one-line mapping and a pointer rather than a copy — each of those files already said "don't restate it here", and BP-041 says it is an index entry, not a second copy.
