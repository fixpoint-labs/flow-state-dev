## 1. TLDR

**Plain-language summary.** Right now the trading desk knows what you own and what it's worth, but the moment you sell something it forgets the profit or loss on that sale. This adds a durable record of every sale's gain or loss — split into the "short-term vs long-term" buckets that decide the tax rate — broken down by holding and by year. On top of that it adds a lightweight tax profile (your filing status and rough income) and a running, current-year estimate of what those gains and your dividends will roughly cost you at tax time. It's an estimate for planning, clearly labeled as not tax advice, and it stays current as you import or record new transactions.

**Technical statement.** Persist per-disposal realized gain/loss records derived from the FIFO lot engine (short/long classified), materialized on the same ledger ingest/void seam as positions; add a year dimension to income aggregation; add a user tax profile and a deterministic current-year federal tax estimate; surface a per-account "Realized Gains" tab and a household tax-estimate card.

**Deliverables** (grouped to mirror the Implementation Sequence, §5):

## 2. Overview

The ledger substrate (FIX-774) reconstructs FIFO lots and open positions from transaction events, and income (dividends/interest) is aggregated at read time. But realized gains are **computed and discarded**: `deriveLots()`'s disposal loop consumes open lots oldest-first and never records the proceeds/basis/gain of the sale. Income has no year dimension, and there is no tax classification (short vs long) or estimate anywhere.

This spec makes realized gains a **first-class, persisted, auditable artifact** — one record per (disposal event × consumed lot), short/long classified — materialized on the exact ledger ingest/void seam that already materializes positions, so it stays live and retracts correctly when an event is voided. It adds a year-dimensioned income read, a small user tax profile, and a deterministic current-year federal tax estimate, surfaced as a per-account Realized Gains tab plus a household tax-estimate card. Everything is a planning estimate, explicitly not filing-grade — the same real-money honesty posture the rest of the desk holds.

## 3. Background & Research

### 3.1 How the codebase already solves the adjacent problems

- **`materializePositions()`** (`repository.ts:287`) is the template. It re-reads an account's full deterministically-ordered event history inside the ingest/void transaction, runs `deriveLots()`, and upserts/deletes/clears holdings rows — a full recompute where "the ledger is the authority." It is called at exactly two sites: after ingest (`repository.ts:629`) and after void (`repository.ts:659`). A realized-gains materializer mirrors this exactly, called immediately after each.
- **`deriveLots()`** (`lots.ts:66`) is a pure BP-019 leaf: an ordered FIFO reduction over the event stream. Its disposal branch (`lots.ts:105–120`) already consumes the exact lots a realized record needs (`lot.costPerShare`, `lot.acquiredDate`) and has the disposal event in scope (`e.tradeDate`, `e.amount`). It discards all of it. This is the seam to extend.
- **`getIncomeSummary()`** (`repository.ts:677`) is a SQL aggregate with a `FILTER`-based dividend/interest split, grouped by `(account, ticker)`, non-voided only. A year dimension is `extract(year from trade_date)` added to select + group-by.
- **`holding-term.ts`** already encodes the IRS ST/LT boundary (`longBoundary()`, `lots.ts` for open lots) — "long once held MORE than one year, anniversary day itself still short." Realized classification reuses the same primitive.
- **`risk-mandate.ts`** (`analysis/lib/`) is the config-as-data precedent: a typed `readonly` pack of presets with a documented INVARIANT and pure resolvers. The tax bracket table mirrors this shape.
- **Tax profile persistence:** the portfolio domain is app-owned Postgres reached through `getRepository()`, and `portfolio-writes.ts:1–24` establishes that domain CRUD is REST routes over the repository, **not** flow actions. A tax profile follows the `accounts` table + repository + REST-route pattern, not a user-scoped FSD resource (which would need a bound session and a flow action).

### 3.2 Industry research (how established tools shape these records)

- **Persisted per-lot records are the accounting-grade norm.** GnuCash's "scrub" creates a capital gain/loss transaction *per lot* consumed by a sale; Beancount materializes the gain into an income posting per reduction. Portfolio trackers that recompute-on-read (Ghostfolio, Portfolio Performance) have produced real aggregation bugs across round-trips. Since we already reconstruct FIFO lots, persisting at disposal time is cheap and auditable — **one row per (disposal × consumed lot)**, each independently ST/LT classifiable (a single sale can be part-short, part-long). This also matches tax reality: Form 8949 splits ST and LT into separate parts even when aggregating a "VARIOUS"-dated block.
- **The precise ST/LT rule.** Long-term = held **more than** one year; exactly one year is short-term. The count is **calendar-anniversary-based, not day-count** (`disposedDate > acquiredDate + 1 year`), using **trade date** on both ends. Do not implement `days >= 365`. This is exactly what `holding-term.ts:longBoundary` already does.
- **Netting (Schedule D order).** Net ST vs ST, LT vs LT, then cross-net; a net loss offsets up to **$3,000/yr** ($1,500 MFS) of ordinary income, excess carries forward retaining character. Steps are trivial and materially change the estimate.
- **Estimate model.** Established rough estimators (Betterment's Tax Impact Preview) use user marginal rates as a deliberate **upper bound** with disclaimers; nobody in this class walks brackets precisely. Chosen approach: **filing status + baked-in 2026 bracket/LTCG tables with user rate overrides** (research "Approach B degrading to A"). ST gains + interest at the marginal ordinary rate; LT gains + dividends at the 0/15/20 LTCG rate looked up from approximate taxable income; user can override either rate (the staleness escape hatch).
- **2026 figures are published** (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, incl. OBBBA) — safe to bake in, versioned by year.
- **Known pitfalls the industry defers** (and so do we, with disclaimers): wash sales (general trackers don't implement — needs cross-account ±30-day substantially-identical matching), return-of-capital basis reduction, qualified-vs-ordinary dividend per-position classification (a single profile-level assumption is standard). NIIT (3.8% over fixed MAGI thresholds) is cheap but needs a MAGI input — deferred to keep the profile lean.

### 3.3 Key Decisions & Ramifications (top 5)

1. **Extend `deriveLots()` to emit a third `disposals` array, rather than a separate `deriveRealizedGains()` walk.**
   - *Alternative rejected:* a sibling pure function re-walking the event stream.
   - *Ramification:* one FIFO reduction is the single source of truth for lot consumption — positions and realized gains can never drift apart. Cost: `deriveLots`'s return type gains a field (callers that ignore it are unaffected; the two current callers — `materializePositions` and the client-side term derivation in `portfolio-pane` — destructure only what they use). Locks in that realized gains are always derived in lockstep with positions.

2. **Realized gains are persisted (materialized table), not recomputed on read.**
   - *Alternative rejected:* derive on every read (the Ghostfolio/Portfolio-Performance approach).
   - *Ramification:* auditable, indexable, one row per (disposal × consumed lot) matching Form 8949 granularity, and consistent-by-construction with the holdings the same transaction wrote. Cost: a new table + migration + a second materializer at the ingest/void sites. Retraction on void is automatic (full recompute). Concurrency safety comes from a **per-account advisory lock** on the ingest/void transaction (serializes same-account recomputes so a concurrent void can't be resurrected by an in-flight import), with a `(disposalEventId, lotIndex)` unique index as defense-in-depth — the derived table isn't keyless. This is the accounting-grade choice and matches the ledger's "materialize inside the transaction" stance.

3. **Realized gains are emitted only on `type === "sell"`; lot consumption stays sign-driven.**
   - *Alternative rejected:* emit a realized record for every share-removing event (naive "capture every disposal").
   - *Ramification:* a `transfer`-out moves shares between your own accounts — it consumes lots (the position leaves) but is **not** a taxable disposition, so it must not produce a gain. Gating emission on `sell` while keeping consumption sign-driven is the correctness line. Locks in that cross-account basis continuity (a transfer carrying its lots to the destination) is out of scope for v1 (destination gets a basis-unknown transfer-in, as today).

4. **The pane reads a single composite `GET /api/portfolio/tax` endpoint** (profile + realized rows + income-by-year + estimate), deviating from the repo's one-route-per-resource convention.
   - *Alternative rejected:* three thin GET routes (realized-gains, income-by-year, tax-estimate) mirroring `income/route.ts`.
   - *Ramification:* the tax pane is a dashboard of four co-rendered derived reads keyed on the same `(userId, year)`; one endpoint means one hook, one refetch, and avoids three near-identical boilerplate routes (a duplication the architecture review flagged). The route fetches profile + realized rows + income-by-year + account types **once** and composes the estimate in-handler via the pure `estimateTaxLiability()` leaf (no repository method that re-queries the same rows). Cost: a composite handler is less RESTful and mixes concerns; the profile **write** stays its own `PUT /api/portfolio/tax-profile`. Flagged for review sign-off (see Open Questions).

5. **Tax figures stay "display approximation" (JS-number, RISK-P5), not exact-decimal.**
   - *Alternative rejected:* read `numeric` columns as strings and do decimal math for the estimate.
   - *Ramification:* consistent with the whole app's money posture (`repository.ts:178–184` narrows `numeric` → JS double at the read boundary) and adequate for a labeled estimate. Cost: the estimate is explicitly not filing-grade — reinforced by the disclaimer. Revisiting exact-decimal is a documented follow-up if a filing-grade export is ever built.

### 4.2 Data flow

**Write path (unchanged seam, extended):**
```
record manual event / import file  →  ingestLedgerEvents(events, userId)   [one tx]
   → dedup + insert ledger rows
   → for each touched account:  materializePositions(tx, id)               (existing)
                                materializeRealizedGains(tx, id)            (NEW — right after)
void event  →  voidLedgerEvents(...)  [one tx]
   → tombstone rows
   → materializePositions(tx, accountId) + materializeRealizedGains(tx, accountId)   (NEW)
```
`materializeRealizedGains(tx, accountId)`: DELETE all `realized_gains` rows for the account, re-read ordered events, `deriveLots(rows).disposals`, INSERT the fresh rows with `onConflictDoNothing` on the `(disposalEventId, lotIndex)` unique key. Full recompute — a void retracts gains, a re-ingest is idempotent. **Concurrency:** the ingest/void transaction takes a **per-account advisory lock** (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`) before materializing (see §4.4) — the unique key alone does NOT prevent a concurrent void's row from being resurrected by an in-flight import that read pre-void state. The lock serializes same-account recomputes so exactly one final derivation runs over the committed ledger. **Implementer note:** do NOT copy `materializePositions`'s early-return (`repository.ts:308`, `if (activeTickers.size === 0 && voidedOnlyTickers.size === 0) return`). Realized gains must ALWAYS delete-then-reinsert; an all-voided account whose rows early-returned would keep stale realized rows (a retraction bug). The delete is unconditional.

**Read path (tax pane):**
```
GET /api/portfolio/tax?userId&year        (year scopes ONLY the estimate; realized/income rows are ALL-year)
   → repo.getTaxProfile(userId)
   → repo.getRealizedGains(userId, {})                  (ALL-year per-disposal rows, all accounts — for the by-year tab)
   → repo.getIncomeSummaryByYear(userId, {})            (ALL-year income — for the by-year view)
   → repo.getAccountsForUser(userId)                    (account type + currency map)
   → estimateTaxLiability({ profile, year, <taxable-account USD-row gains for `year` summed by term>,
                            <taxable-account USD-row income for `year` summed> })   (pure leaf, in-handler)
   → { profile, realizedGains, incomeByYear, estimate }
```
Two things the route does deliberately (both flagged in review):
- **`year` scopes only the estimate, not the returned rows.** `realizedGains` and `incomeByYear` come back for ALL years so the Realized Gains tab and income view can show prior-year history (desired outcome #1, "for any past year"); the client rolls up / offers a year selector. Only the current-year (default `year`) slice feeds the tax estimate.
- **The estimate filters BOTH gains AND income to taxable accounts** — using the same `accounts` type map, keep only `type === "taxable"` rows before summing gains AND before summing dividends/interest. Otherwise IRA/Roth/401k dividends leak into `ordinaryTaxable`/`ltcgTaxable` and overstate the total. It also filters to a **single currency by the LEDGER-ROW `currency`** (each realized-gain / income row carries its source event's currency — OFX `CURDEF` / manual field — which can differ from `account.currency`): keep only rows with `currency === "USD"`; a non-USD row is excluded with an `assumptions` note (multi-currency is a Non-Goal — the estimate must not sum EUR and USD as bare numbers). Filtering on `account.currency` alone would wrongly admit EUR rows held in a default-USD account.

The estimate is composed in the route handler from the rows already fetched via the pure `estimateTaxLiability()` leaf. No `getTaxEstimate` repository method (it would re-run the same queries the composite read already ran). The per-account Realized Gains tab filters `realizedGains` to the account and rolls up by ticker/year/term client-side (`realized-gains-row-model.ts`, `useMemo`). The tax-estimate card renders `estimate`.

### 4.3 API surface (exact signatures)

**Pure derivation** (`lots.ts` / `realized-gains.ts`):
```ts
/** One realized disposal: a sale's gain against ONE consumed FIFO lot (or the
 *  unmatched remainder of an over-sell, which has no lot → unknown acquisition). */
// TWO INDEPENDENT provenance axes — do not conflate them:
//   (1) acquisition-date known?  drives `acquiredDate` / `term`
//   (2) amount realized known?   drives `costBasis`/`gain` (basis side) and proceeds side
// A no-price buy has a KNOWN date but UNKNOWN basis; a transfer-in has a KNOWN
// basis (if the broker gave a price) but an UNKNOWN date. Both must be modeled.
export type RealizedDisposal = {
  ticker: string;
  disposedDate: string;              // sell event tradeDate (YYYY-MM-DD)
  acquiredDate: string | null;       // consumed lot's acquisition date, or NULL when unknowable:
                                     // ANY transfer-in lot (its date is the transfer date, not the
                                     // original acquisition — even when cost basis IS known), or an
                                     // unmatched over-sell. A buy (even a no-price buy) has a real date.
  quantity: number;                  // shares consumed from THIS lot (or unmatched shares)
  proceeds: number | null;           // pro-rata allocation of the sell's amount; NULL when the sell's
                                     // proceeds are unknown (importer placeholder — see below)
  costBasis: number | null;          // quantity × lot.costPerShare; null when basis unknown
  gain: number | null;               // proceeds − costBasis; NULL when EITHER proceeds OR basis unknown
  term: "short" | "long" | "unknown";// classifyTerm(...); "unknown" iff acquiredDate is null
  basisUnknown: string | null;       // reason string (mirrors the ledger's basisUnknown /
                                     // LedgerRow), null when basis is known — NOT a boolean,
                                     // so it round-trips the text column and the RealizedGainRow type
  disposalEventId: string;           // the sell ledger event id (provenance)
  lotIndex: number;                  // 0-based ordinal of the consumed lot within this sell
                                     // (deterministic FIFO order); the unmatched remainder
                                     // gets the next index. Part of the derived-row identity.
};

// deriveLots return type gains a third field:
export function deriveLots(events: LedgerRow[]): {
  positions: DerivedPosition[];
  lots: Lot[];
  disposals: RealizedDisposal[]; // NEW — one per (sell event × consumed lot), plus one per
                                 // unmatched over-sell remainder
};
```
**Lot provenance (in `deriveLots`).** The internal `Lot` must carry `acquisitionDateKnown: boolean` — `true` for a `buy` (its `tradeDate` IS the acquisition), `false` for a `transfer`-in (its `tradeDate` is only when it arrived in this account). When a lot is consumed, the realized row's `acquiredDate` is the lot date only if `acquisitionDateKnown`, else `null`. This is separate from cost-basis provenance: a transfer-in with a broker-supplied price has a known basis but still an unknown acquisition date. **The same nulling applies to the RETURNED open `lots` / `positions`, not just consumed disposals** — the Holdings tab renders its term column from `deriveLots(...).lots` (`portfolio-pane.tsx:152`), so an *unsold* transfer-in position must expose `acquiredDate: null` too, or `holding-term.ts` would render a short/long term off the transfer date. (This changes the existing open-position term rendering for transfer-ins from "computed off transfer date" to "unknown" — a deliberate correctness change, noted in the Non-Goals/limitations.)

`classifyTerm(acquiredDate: string | null, disposedDate): "short" | "long" | "unknown"` in `holding-period.ts` — returns `"unknown"` when `acquiredDate` is null, else `longBoundary`-based.

**The unknown cases (surfaced, not dropped — honest-basis posture):**
- **transfer-in lot consumed** — `acquiredDate: null`, `term: "unknown"` (regardless of whether cost basis is known); `costBasis`/`gain` follow the basis axis.
- **no-price / no-total buy lot consumed** — the buy's date IS known so `term` is real (short/long), but `costBasis: null` → `gain: null`. Known term, unknown gain.
- **proceeds-unknown sell** — a sell whose proceeds the source couldn't supply (the OFX importer's no-`TOTAL`/`UNITPRICE` placeholder) must be carried by an **explicit `proceedsUnknown` marker on the ledger event, NOT inferred from `amount === 0`** (the value heuristic also matches a genuine manual $0 / worthless-security sale — a real disposal, proceeds 0, gain = −basis). **This marker must be wired end-to-end** (it does not exist in FIX-774 today): add a nullable `proceedsUnknown` (a reason string, mirroring `basisUnknown`) to `ledgerEventInputSchema`, `LedgerRow`, and the `app.ledger_events` table (a column in this spec's migration), set by the importer's placeholder path. It is **deliberately EXCLUDED from the content fingerprint** (`computeFingerprint`) — it is derivation metadata, not row identity, so the FIX-774 fingerprint contract is unchanged. A marked proceeds-unknown sell → `proceeds: null`, `gain: null`; an unmarked $0 sell is a real $0-proceeds disposal.
- **malformed sell guard (ingest)** — `deriveLots` is sign-driven, so a manual `{ type: "sell", quantity: 10 }` (positive qty) or `{ type: "sell", quantity: null }` / no ticker would be mis-treated as an acquisition and never realize a gain. `recordManualEvent` therefore **validates at the zod boundary that a `sell` has a non-null `ticker` and a negative `quantity`** (and `amount >= 0`) — a malformed sell is a 400, not silent bad data feeding the tax derivation.
- **unmatched over-sell** (no open lot) — `acquiredDate: null`, `term: "unknown"`, `costBasis: null`, `gain: null`, real `proceeds`, `basisUnknown: "no-acquisition-lot"`.

Proceeds allocation: each row's `proceeds = (rowQty / totalSellQty) × sellEvent.amount` when proceeds are known; `amount` (validated `>= 0` for sells) is the amount realized (net cash), fee already reflected. **Taxable-bucket rule (load-bearing):** a disposal contributes to the ST/LT taxable buckets **only when `gain !== null` AND `term !== "unknown"`**. Any disposal with `gain === null` **OR** `term === "unknown"` is excluded from the buckets and counted in `basisUnknownProceeds`/`basisUnknownCount` — so a known-date/null-gain no-price-buy sale and a known-gain/unknown-term transfer-in sale are both handled, not just `term === "unknown"` rows.

  // so a figure that already includes them double-counts. The dialog must label it

export const taxProfileInputSchema = taxProfileInputBase.refine(taxProfileRefine, taxProfileRefineMsg);
export type TaxProfileInput = z.infer<typeof taxProfileInputSchema>;

// tax-tables.ts (config-as-data, risk-mandate.ts shape)
export const TAX_YEAR = 2026;
export const TAX_TABLE_SOURCE = "Rev. Proc. 2025-32";
// Ordinary rate: stackingIncome = base income + the ORDINARY bucket only
// (`taxableIncome + ordinaryTaxable`). Preferential income sits ABOVE ordinary for
// rate purposes and must NOT push the ordinary bucket into a higher ordinary bracket.
export function resolveMarginalOrdinaryRate(status, stackingIncome): number;  // fraction 0..0.37
// LTCG rate: stackingIncome = base income + ordinary bucket + preferential bucket

export function resolveLtcgRate(status, stackingIncome): 0 | 0.15 | 0.20;

// tax-estimate.ts
export type TaxEstimate = {
  year: number; tableSource: string;
  netShortTerm: number; netLongTerm: number;   // after ST/LT netting + cross-net (may be negative)
  ordinaryTaxable: number;                       // max(0, net ST) + interest, less the capped loss deduction
  ltcgTaxable: number;                           // max(0, net LT capital GAIN) + qualified dividends
                                                 // (a capital LOSS never reduces the dividend tax)
  deductibleLossThisYear: number;                // capped at 3000/1500
  lossCarryforward: number;                       // display only
  estimatedFederal: number; estimatedState: number; estimatedTotal: number;
  effectiveOrdinaryRate: number; effectiveLtcgRate: number;
  basisUnknownProceeds: number; basisUnknownCount: number; // honesty: excluded from taxable
  assumptions: string[];                          // rendered as caveats
  mandateBlind?: never;                           // (n/a)
};
export function estimateTaxLiability(input: {
  profile: TaxProfileInput | null;                // NULL = no profile saved → zeros + "enter your profile"
                                                  // assumption, never throws (the route need not fabricate inputs)
  year: number;                                   // requested tax year — sets TaxEstimate.year + the stale-table caveat
  shortGains: number; longGains: number;          // summed gain of taxable-account USD-row disposals THIS year
                                                  // with gain !== null AND term !== "unknown" (rest → basisUnknown*)
  dividends: number; interest: number;            // this year, taxable-account USD rows
  basisUnknownProceeds: number; basisUnknownCount: number;  // gain===null OR term==="unknown" disposals
}): TaxEstimate;
```
Estimate methodology (each point below was review-flagged — the math has to be unambiguous):
- **Rates are fractions internally.** `resolveMarginalOrdinaryRate` / `resolveLtcgRate` return fractions (e.g. `0.22`). The profile's `*RatePct` overrides are stored on the **0..100** scale, so an override is **divided by 100** before it becomes an `effective*Rate` — a `22` override must tax at `0.22×`, never `22×`.
- **Netting + loss cap (Schedule D order).** Net ST vs ST, LT vs LT, cross-net. The **taxable capital-gain components are floored at 0** for the buckets — a net capital LOSS does NOT reduce the ordinary or preferential buckets directly (in particular it must NOT offset qualified dividends, which sit in the preferential bucket). Instead the excess capital loss follows the Schedule D cap: `deductibleLossThisYear = min(netCapitalLoss, 3000 | 1500-MFS)` reduces ordinary income (clamped — a $50k loss reduces ordinary by at most $3k, never a negative estimate / phantom credit), and the remainder is `lossCarryforward` (display only). So `ordinaryTaxable = max(0, max(0, netShortTerm) + interest − deductibleLossThisYear)` (the outer `max(0, …)` matters — a $3k loss deduction against $100 interest must floor at 0, not −2900) and `ltcgTaxable = max(0, max(0, netLongTerm) + qualifiedDividends)` (the outer `max(0, …)` guards a **net-negative dividend reversal** — the income parser preserves negative dividend/interest correction rows, so a reversal-heavy year must floor the preferential bucket at 0, not produce negative tax). Interest can likewise be net-negative; the ordinary outer clamp already covers it.
- **Ordinary bucket** (net ST gains after the capped loss + interest) taxed at `marginalOrdinaryRatePct/100` (override) else `resolveMarginalOrdinaryRate(status, taxableIncome + ordinaryTaxable)` — resolved at income **PLUS the ordinary bucket** (symmetric with LTCG stacking below), so a low base income + large ST sale doesn't apply the bottom bracket to the whole bucket.
- **Preferential bucket** (net LT gains + qualified dividends, `ltcgTaxable`) taxed at `ltcgRatePct/100` (override) else `resolveLtcgRate(status, taxableIncome + ordinaryTaxable + ltcgTaxable)` — LT gains stack on top of **ALL** ordinary income (base income + this year's ST gains + interest, i.e. `taxableIncome + ordinaryTaxable`), then the preferential bucket sits above that. Resolving at the top of the full stack means a low base income with large same-year ST gains/interest can't hand the LT bucket a 0% rate the ordinary income has already filled past. A single blended rate per bucket is the deliberate v1 simplification over full layer-by-layer bracket stacking (noted in `assumptions`).
- **State** — the optional flat `stateRatePct/100` applies to the **full taxable bucket** (`ordinaryTaxable + ltcgTaxable`), not gains alone, since it's a flat state rate and dividends/interest are already in the federal taxable buckets.
- **Year** — `year` is threaded in so the estimate sets `TaxEstimate.year` and adds the "using {TAX_YEAR} brackets" caveat to `assumptions` when `year !== TAX_YEAR` (the route accepts `?year=`; the leaf owns the label).

Deliberate filters, all review-flagged:
- **Taxable predicate is exactly `account.type === "taxable"`** — `IRA`/`Roth`/`401k` are tax-advantaged; applied to BOTH gains AND income (IRA dividends must not inflate the estimate). A future added account type must consciously opt in.

]);

export const taxProfiles = appSchema.table("tax_profiles", {
  userId: text("user_id").primaryKey(),
  filingStatus: text("filing_status").notNull(),    // enum at zod boundary
  taxableIncome: numeric("taxable_income"),
  marginalOrdinaryRatePct: numeric("marginal_ordinary_rate_pct"),
  ltcgRatePct: numeric("ltcg_rate_pct"),
  stateRatePct: numeric("state_rate_pct"),
  updatedAt: timestamp("updated_at", { withTimezone: true, mode: "string" }).notNull().default(sql`now()`),
});
```
**Concurrency — a per-account serialization lock, plus a deterministic unique index as defense-in-depth.** The table is fully recomputed (delete-all + re-insert per account) inside the ingest/void transaction. Two guards, because one alone is insufficient (a reviewer's executable repro showed the unique key does NOT by itself make retraction safe):
- **`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('realized:' || accountId))` at the top of the `ingestLedgerEvents` / `voidLedgerEvents` transaction** — the actual serialization. Without it, an import whose ledger-events SELECT ran *before* a concurrent void committed re-derives the just-voided sell's realized row and re-inserts it after the void deleted it (a resurrected stale row — `onConflictDoNothing` can't catch it, because a deleted-then-reinserted row is new, not a conflict). Serializing per-account recomputes closes this. Since **every** ledger writer (manual, FIX-775 file import, FIX-853 Plaid sync) already funnels through this one transaction, the lock lives there once (BP-028 — fix at the owning layer). It also retroactively hardens `materializePositions`, which shares the same delete/upsert-without-lock exposure; take the lock before both materializers.
  - **Ordered locking (deadlock-free).** `ingestLedgerEvents` touches a *set* of accounts (its `accountIds`), so it must acquire the per-account locks in a **deterministic order — sort `accountIds` ascending, then `pg_advisory_xact_lock` each in that order**, and run both materializers over accounts in the same sorted order. Two batches touching `{A,B}` and `{B,A}` that locked in input/iteration order could otherwise deadlock before either materializes. `voidLedgerEvents` is single-account, so this is only load-bearing for the multi-account ingest path — but the sort is unconditional so a future multi-account void inherits it.
- **The `(disposalEventId, lotIndex)` unique index** — defense-in-depth for the empty-table double-insert window and a guard against a buggy caller that skips the lock; the materializer re-inserts with `onConflictDoNothing`. It is NOT the primary concurrency guarantee — the advisory lock is.

Migration generated via `pnpm db:generate` (drizzle-kit, `schemaFilter: ["app"]`). **Generate it last, and take whatever number drizzle-kit assigns on the actual base tree** — do not hardcode `0003`. On a tree containing #659's `0002` it becomes `0003`; if this lands before #659, it is `0002` and #659 (or this) must regenerate. Coordinate so the journal/snapshot chain stays linear (see Dependencies).

### 4.5 Error handling

- **Ownership** — `getRealizedGains`/`getIncomeSummaryByYear`/`getTaxProfile` (and the `tax` route that composes them) are `userId`-scoped reads; a foreign `userId` returns that user's (empty) data, never another's. `upsertTaxProfile` keys on `userId` (PK). Same posture as the existing reads.
- **Null tax profile** — a valid state; the estimate returns zeros + an "enter your profile" assumption line, never throws.
- **Basis-unknown / unknown-acquisition disposals** — `costBasis: null`, `gain: null`, and `term: "unknown"` when the acquisition date is unknown (basis-unknown transfer-in, or unmatched over-sell). Excluded from the ST/LT taxable buckets, surfaced as `basisUnknownProceeds`/`basisUnknownCount`. Never treated as zero basis (overstates gain) and never guessed into a term from a transfer date (fabricates a holding period) — the honest discipline `lots.ts` holds.
- **Unmatched disposal shares** (a sell with no prior lot — partial import) — the unmatched remainder **emits** a realized row (acquisition/term/basis unknown, real proceeds) so the taxable sale is visible, rather than disappearing. `materializePositions` still keeps the snapshot holdings row basis-cleared (existing behavior).
- **Stale bracket table** — if `year` isn't the baked-in `TAX_YEAR`, fall back to the latest table with an `assumptions` note ("using 2026 brackets"); user rate overrides bypass the table entirely.
- **Bad profile input** — rejected at the route's zod boundary (`.finite()` on rates/income) with a 400, before the repository.

## 6. Edge Cases & Error Handling

| Case | Expected behavior |
|---|---|
| Sell spanning 2 lots, one <1yr one >1yr | Two realized records, one `short` one `long`, proceeds allocated pro-rata by consumed qty |
| Exactly one year held | `short` (boundary exclusive — matches `longBoundary`) |
| Leap-year anniversary | Calendar arithmetic via `longBoundary`; no `days>=365` |
| Transfer-in lot sold (basis known OR not) | `acquiredDate: null`, `term: "unknown"` (transfer date ≠ acquisition — no fabricated holding period), regardless of whether cost is known; excluded from ST/LT buckets, counted in `basisUnknownProceeds` |
| No-price/no-total **buy** lot sold | Date IS known → `term` real (short/long), but `costBasis: null` → `gain: null`; still excluded from buckets (rule is `gain===null` OR `term==="unknown"`), counted in `basisUnknownProceeds` |
| Proceeds-unknown sell (importer placeholder `amount:0`, no unitPrice) | `proceeds: null`, `gain: null` — NOT taxed as a $0 sale (which would fabricate a full loss); excluded, counted |
| `transfer`-out (negative qty) | Consumes lots (position leaves) but emits **no** realized record (not a taxable disposition) |
| Sell with no prior lot / over-sell (qty exceeds open lots) | The matched portion emits normal records; the **unmatched remainder emits a realized row** with `acquiredDate: null`, `term: "unknown"`, `costBasis: null`, `gain: null`, real `proceeds` — surfaced in the Realized Gains tab and `basisUnknownProceeds`, NOT dropped (dropping would understate sales and tax impact for incomplete broker history). The holdings row is still kept basis-cleared (existing `materializePositions` behavior) |
| Void a sell | `materializeRealizedGains` re-derives; the sale's realized rows disappear (retraction) |
| Void a buy later consumed by a sell | Full recompute re-matches the sell to remaining lots (or leaves it unmatched); rows recompute |
| IRA/Roth/401k account | Realized rows materialized + shown in the account tab (informational); **excluded** from the tax estimate |
| Net capital loss | ST/LT netted + cross-net; taxable capital-gain components floored at 0 (a loss never reduces the ordinary/preferential buckets or offsets qualified dividends); up to $3,000 ($1,500 MFS) offsets ordinary income; remainder carryforward (not applied to future years) |
| Net capital loss + qualified dividends | Dividends still taxed at the LTCG rate — the loss can't wipe them (Schedule D cap) |
| No tax profile | Estimate returns zeros + "enter your profile" assumption; never throws |
| `year` not in baked-in table | Fall back to latest table + `assumptions` note; user overrides bypass |
| Dividends assumed qualified | Taxed at LTCG rate with a documented assumption line (per-position qualification not modeled) |

**Error taxonomy.** All paths are deterministic reads/writes — no retryable/fatal LLM taxonomy. Bad input → 400 at the route zod boundary. Ownership violations → empty/no-op (userId-scoped). Basis/matching gaps → surfaced honestly (null gain, excluded), never fabricated.

## 8. Non-Goals

- **Wash sales** — no ±30-day substantially-identical disallowed-loss adjustment (general trackers defer this; needs cross-account matching). Named in the estimate disclaimer.
- **Specific-lot / LIFO / HIFO selection** — FIFO only (the IRS default, matches `deriveLots`). Specific-ID is a separate, larger feature.
- **Corporate-action basis** — splits / return-of-capital not modeled; ROC distributions overstate income and later basis. Disclaimer notes it.
- **NIIT (3.8%)** — deferred (needs a MAGI input); named in the disclaimer as excluded.
- **Per-position qualified-vs-ordinary dividend classification** — a single profile-level assumption (dividends treated as qualified) with a caveat, not the 121-day-window computation.
- **State-specific bracket tables** — flat optional state rate only (answers issue OQ #2: federal-first with a flat state input).
- **Loss carryforward INTO future years** — displayed, not applied to next year's estimate.
- **Multi-currency** — single-currency per account (inherited ledger limitation).
- **Non-equity asset tax treatment** — bonds/options/MMF classified but not tax-differentiated (FIX-773).
- **Cross-account basis continuity on transfers** — a transfer-out doesn't carry its lots to the destination (destination is a basis-unknown transfer-in, as today).
- **Household-level tax view** — the estimate card is user-scoped and standalone; relocating it into FIX-762's household Health view is deferred (see Open Questions / Dependencies). FIX-762 is a sibling, not a blocker.

**Deepening opportunities flagged (follow-ups via `fsd:improve-codebase-architecture`, not this spec):**
- Duplicated thin REST-route + hook wiring (`income`/`ledger`/tax routes are near-identical `userId`-guarded wrappers). The composite `GET /api/portfolio/tax` avoids adding three more copies but the underlying route+hook boilerplate is ripe for a shared helper.
- `materializePositions` is an 84-line four-branch function; realized-gains materialization is added as a **separate** peer function (not bolted on) to keep single responsibility — but the pair invites a shared "materialize on ingest/void" registration seam later.
