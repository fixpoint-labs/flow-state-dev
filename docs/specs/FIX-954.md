## Summary

**The problem.** Look-through — seeing inside an ETF so that Apple held directly
and Apple held through SPY add up — is arithmetically correct but produced an
unusable screen. Against a real 44-ETF book it shows two sector panels that
appear to disagree, a holdings table that silently hides most of its rows, a stat
reading `1.5–338.1`, and forty funds mashed into one run-on sentence.

**What we're building — two things, nothing else**, both straight from what the
user asked for:

1. **One sector view covering 100% of invested NAV**, with the portion we can't
   see inside drawn as an explicit bar rather than left as a silent gap.
   → *"Sector exposure is supposed to ultimately allow me to see exposure of the
   entire portfolio."*
2. **One holdings view** giving per-symbol totals aggregated across every wrapper
   and every account. → *"how much of each stock I own, regardless if it's in an
   ETF or directly owned across multiple accounts."*

**What we're deliberately not building** (§6): sector→symbol drill-down, a basis
toggle, composition bars, a fine-grained residual taxonomy, and the `Effective
positions` statistic.

**The key finding** (§0.1): the two sector panels people read as contradictory
are already on the **same denominator**. The look-through bars simply stop short
of 100% because the remainder is never drawn. A missing bar, not broken math.

**Scope and sequencing.** Both phases are FIX-954; the split is about PR
boundaries, not separate issues. **Phase 1** (~half a day, no contract change)
extracts one shared reason classifier, fixes the holdings table and the
statement-basis truncation, and — via a residual line inside the existing
look-through sector block — makes a first honest dent in the sector complaint.
**Phase 2** (~3 days) adds two leaf fields, deletes the null-out, brings the
analysis prompt to parity, and lands the unified panel. Phase 1 ships first
because it needs no contract change, not because it is different work.

## 0. Findings from the code

These are what make the rest credible. Two of them changed the design.

### 0.1 The two sector panels share one denominator

Both are `% of invested NAV` — `portfolio-health.ts:383` and
`etf-look-through.ts:965` are the identical `pctOf(marketValue, investedNav)`
call. What differs is **completeness, not scale**: the wrapper bars sum to ~100%,
the look-through bars sum to `sectorCoveragePct`, and the remainder sits in
`sectorResidual.sharePct` — a field **the leaf already computes and the UI never
renders** (type at `:252`, computed at ~`:974`).

So the panels aren't contradictory answers on incomparable scales. They're the
same scale at two resolutions: full mass at coarse resolution (wrapper, where
"Funds" is one opaque block) versus fine resolution at partial mass
(look-through). Unifying them is arithmetic, not reconciliation — draw the
attributed bars plus the residual and it closes to 100% by construction, with no
renormalization.

What the user experienced is worse than two denominators: two panels on the
*same* scale, one of which silently doesn't add up. That reads as broken math.

**The "57.6% vs 22.7%" confusion** is cross-*axis*, not cross-denominator:
`Funds (no look-through)` is a wrapper **sector** bucket; `22.7%` is the
look-through **name**-axis coverage. Labelled alike, typeset alike, two blocks
apart, not comparable.

### 0.2 Sector → constituent symbols is not derivable for fund-held mass

Direct positions: the symbol is available today (`pos.ticker` is in scope at
`etf-look-through.ts:642`, just not retained). The wrapper basis already exposes
it — `sectorExposure[].constituents` carries tickers per bucket.

Fund-attributed mass: the symbols **do not exist**. Decision 7 (`:35-37`) takes
the sector axis from the fund's own *aggregate* reported allocation.
`fp.sectors` (`{sector, weight}`) and `fp.constituents` (`{ticker, weight}`) are
independent, unlinked profile fields — nothing joins "SPY contributed 2.4% to
Technology" to a ticker. Deriving it would reverse Decision 7, need
classifications for hundreds of unseen tickers, and yield a sector total that
disagrees with the fund's own reported allocation.

**This is why the sector drill-down is cut** (§6): we could only ever half-answer
"what symbols are in what sector," and the holdings view answers the underlying
need better.

### 0.3 "Effective positions" is a label collision

`health-section.tsx:260` renders `label="Effective positions"` for the wrapper
point estimate (`1/HHI`); `:430` renders the same label for the look-through
interval. Same label, twice, one scroll, different bases, wildly different values.

The user read it literally and asked for something else entirely — *"how much of
each stock I own"* — which inverse-HHI does not measure. A label that reliably
makes people expect a different quantity is a defective label, and the quantity
they want is outcome 2. **Cut from the UI**; retained in the leaf and the analysis
prompt, where a model can use the interval's width.

### 0.4 The holdings table has no account provenance

`LookThroughPositionInput` (`:182-188`) carries no `accounts`.
`portfolio-health.ts:397` builds it from `[...merged.values()]`, already
ticker-merged, so **"how much" is already correct across accounts** — but
`m.accounts`, in scope on that very line, is dropped at the leaf boundary.
`EffectiveNamePosition.sources` carries `{from, marketValue}` only.

Net: the table answers *how much* ✓ and *through which wrapper* ✓, never *in
which account* ✗. That gap is half of outcome 2, and the only genuinely new data
path in this spec.

### 0.5 There is already a shared opaque-reason classifier — use it

`build-portfolio-context.ts` defines `UNAVAILABLE_REASONS` (a 4-element set:
`"no stored profile"`, `"quota"`, `"transient"`,
`CONSTITUENT_EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE_REASON`) and `classifyOpaqueFunds`, which tags
each entry `unavailable: f.axis === "both" && UNAVAILABLE_REASONS.has(f.reason)`.

Two consequences, both simplifying:

- **It is a total function over any reason string.** Anything not in the set —
  including every parameterized reason — falls to the default branch. **No prefix
  or substring matching is needed anywhere**, which removes the interim UI
  classifier an earlier draft of this spec proposed.
- **The UI must adopt this exact split rather than invent its own**, or the pane
  and the analysis prompt will disagree about which funds are still awaited — the
  precise failure this redesign exists to end.

**Every reason family the leaf emits**, enumerated so nothing is grouped by
accident. An earlier draft's "match the constants, prefix-match the rest" hedge
risked putting permanently-excluded and refreshable funds in the same bucket —
the one distinction the residual split exists to make.

| Group | Reasons | Axis | Subtracted from the ceiling? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awaiting data** *(the whole set)* | `"no stored profile"` · `"quota"` · `"transient"` · `CONSTITUENT_EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE_REASON` | both | no |
| Not attributable — policy | `MUTUAL_FUND_…` · `FIXED_INCOME_…` · `"leveraged/inverse fund"` · `"ineligible"` · `` `fund-of-funds: N% …` `` | both | **yes** |
| Not attributable — data | `"not_an_etf"` · `"malformed"` · `` `holdings data incomplete/malformed (…)` `` · `` `sector data incomplete/malformed (…)` `` | both / names / sectors | no |
| Not attributable — structural | `"no resolvable name-axis constituent (…)"` | names | no |

The classifier's `axis === "both"` condition does real work: every awaiting-data
reason short-circuits as a combined `both` entry before the per-axis logic runs,
so a `names`- or `sectors`-only entry can never be awaiting data — structural, not
incidental.

**One conflict, resolved by conforming.** An earlier draft grouped data-quality
refusals with *awaiting data* (reasoning that a thin profile can heal on the next
~30-day refresh). The existing classifier groups them with structural exclusions,
on the documented rationale that an unknown reason is likelier structural than
transient. The existing classifier wins — conformance over taste, and forking it
is exactly the pane/prompt divergence this redesign exists to end. The cost is
that the *not attributable* group holds some mass that could still resolve, so
only the policy row is permanent enough to move a ceiling (§2.1).

### 0.6 The classifier sizes opaque funds; it does not see the whole residual

Two independent mechanisms put mass in the residual, and only one of them is a
refusal the classifier ever reads:

- **Whole-fund opacity.** A fund the leaf refused on an axis contributes its
  entire market value, with a reason string — `opaqueFunds[]`, classified above.
- **The unreported remainder of a fund that *passed*.** Once a profile clears
  the coverage floor and reconciles, the leaf still adds
  `(1 − Math.min(actualSectorSum, 1)) * mv` to `sectorResidualMass`
  (`etf-look-through.ts:942`; the name axis does the same at `:852`). That mass
  has **no `opaqueFunds` entry and no reason string** — the fund was accepted.

The UI derives the second by subtraction — `sectorResidual.marketValue − Σ` the
market value of the funds opaque on that axis — so it carries no per-fund identity
and its sub-line names no tickers (§2.1).

**It belongs in *awaiting data*, not *not attributable*.** The methodology is
explicit that a thin profile is stored rather than refused and that "coverage can
improve on the next refresh" ([`etf-look-through.md`](../../labs/trading-desk/docs/etf-look-through.md),
"The eligibility predicate and the coverage floor"). Nothing in the code makes a
partial allocation permanent, so nothing may subtract it from a terminal ceiling.

### 0.7 Decision 2, revised for the sector axis

Decision 2 kept the wrapper basis untouched with look-through strictly additive.
This spec revises it **for the sector axis only**: one sector panel, on the
look-through basis.

Decision 2 protected the honesty invariants by *physical separation* — but
separation was a proxy for the requirement, not the requirement. The unified
panel enforces them structurally: the residual is a bar on the axis, so the axis
can't be read without reading it. Wrapper-basis fields, the sizing gates, and the
two-state `lookThrough` contract are all unchanged.

## 1. Principles

**P1 — One question, one axis, one denominator.** One sector axis, `% of invested
NAV`, summing to 100%. The name axis and the sector axis never share a panel,
meter, or stat row.

**P2 — The unknown is a bar, never a gap.** Uncovered mass renders at true size on
the same scale. Never renormalized, never spread pro-rata across sectors. Every
sector bar reads *"at least"* — the residual beside it is the disclaimer.

**P3 — "Can't" and "haven't yet" look different.** The only reason-detail the
view owes the user, and it uses the existing classifier (§0.5).

**P4 — Diagnostic, never a gate.** Look-through doesn't move sizing. No warning
colours for low coverage — incompleteness is not a fault condition.

Reading order: **how complete → what we know → what we don't → what it isn't
for.** The lower-bound sentence sits above every number it qualifies; the scope
note stays at the bottom. Splitting today's single trailing paragraph by function
is the whole fix for it.

**The meter reads `sectorCoveragePct`, not `coveragePct`.** An earlier draft put
the name-axis figure above sector bars totalling 37.4% and was caught in review —
reproducing the exact cross-axis confusion §0.1 exists to eliminate. If the two
axes ever share a panel again, that is the bug.

The residual bar is pinned last regardless of size — at 62.6% it would otherwise
sort to the top and read as the leading exposure rather than the boundary of
knowledge. Sector bars carry **no expand affordance** (§6).

### 2.1 The residual, expanded

```
│    │ ◇ Not attributable                               28.2%    $423k   │ │
│    │   Never decompose by design (18.0%, $270k)                        │ │
│    │     BNDX GBIL LQD SCHP SHY SHYG TLT VCSH VWOB · VTSAX VFIAX · TQQQ│ │
│    │   Holdings data too thin to trust (10.2%, $153k) — VXUS SCHF      │ │
│    │     Re-checked when the stored profile refreshes (~30 days).      │ │
│    │   Attribution tops out near 82% for this book.                    │ │
│    │                                                                   │ │
│    │ ○ Awaiting data — may improve on refresh         34.4%    $516k   │ │
│    │   Never fetched (12.4%, $186k) — IEFA IEMG ACWX                   │ │
│    │     5 profiles fetch per visit; reload to pull the next batch.    │ │
│    │   Funds we read that reported only part of their allocation       │ │
│    │     (22.0%, $330k) — re-checked on the next ~30-day refresh.      │ │
```

**Two groups, not four**, split on the only question the user has — *will this
improve on its own?* Per-fund reason strings stay available as row tooltips; they
don't get their own top-level groups.

**The group is named *awaiting data*, not *pending*** — *pending* names only the
queued fetch, and the group also holds a fund already read whose provider reported
a partial allocation (§0.6). *Awaiting data* covers both, promises no timeline,
and can't be misread as a tense variant of *not attributable*.

**The ceiling is `100 − policy`.** Only the policy row can never resolve
regardless of refresh, so it is the only mass a terminal figure may subtract:
**82.0%** for this book. A prior revision subtracted the partial-allocation mass
too and stated **60.0%** — telling the user to stop waiting on $330k the next
refresh may attribute. Thin-data funds are excluded for the same reason — which is
also why the group's own 28.2% doesn't imply a 71.8% ceiling. Phrase it "tops out
near 82%": a fund that resolves out of *awaiting data* into a policy exclusion
adds to the subtraction, so a stated ceiling is itself an upper bound and only
ever moves down.

**Reload copy must not over-promise.** Of the four awaiting-data *reasons* only
`"no stored profile"` is fetched on the next page load; the rest honour their own
`retryAt` backoff. Scope "reload to pull the next batch" to the never-fetched
sub-line, and give the others honest timing (quota resets daily; transient retries
within ~15 min). The partial-allocation and thin-data sub-lines both carry the
~30-day stored-profile refresh note (7 days after a malformed refusal) — a stored
profile is not refetched just because someone reloaded.

**That timing detail does not argue for a third group.** The split answers *will
this improve on its own?* — yes for everything in *awaiting data*, no for the
policy row, and *not proven either way* for thin data, which conforms to the
existing classifier (§0.5) and is kept out of the ceiling. Backoff windows change
*when*, not *whether*; that's a copy problem, fixed by the per-sub-line notes. A
third group would split on *how soon*, which nobody asked.

### 2.2 Worked-example key

All wireframe numbers come from one illustrative book (not the user's actual
figures). Invested NAV **$1,500,000**.

| Axis | Figure | % | $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sector** | attributed (`sectorCoveragePct`) | 37.4% | $561,000 |
| | residual — not attributable | 28.2% | $423,000 |
| | residual — awaiting data | 34.4% | $516,000 |
| | *terminal ceiling* (`100 − policy`) | *82.0%* | *$1,230,000* |
| **Name** | attributed (`coveragePct`) | 22.6% | $339,000 |
| | residual | 77.4% | $1,161,000 |

Both axes close to 100% independently. The attributed figures differ (37.4% vs
22.6%) because they are **different measurements** — a fund can clear the
coverage floor for sectors and fail it for names, or the reverse. That divergence
is correct and expected, and is exactly why the two axes never share a meter.

## 3. Outcome 2 — the holdings view

**The canonical answer to "how much do I own, and where."** Not the sector panel,
and not a diversification statistic.

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ EFFECTIVE HOLDINGS — across every wrapper and account                     │
│ Largest: NVDA at least 7.4%          Name coverage 22.6% · $339k          │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ticker            Effective weight     Value      Where                   │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ▾ NVDA                     7.4%      $111,000     Direct + VTI, QQQ       │
│     Direct · Taxable       3.1%       $46,500                             │
│     via VTI · Taxable      3.7%       $55,500                             │
│     via QQQ · Roth IRA     0.6%        $9,000                             │
│ ▸ AAPL                     3.1%       $46,500     Direct + 4              │
│ ▸ MSFT                     2.9%       $43,500     VTI, SPY, QQQ           │
│ ▸ AMZN                     2.2%       $33,000     VTI, SPY                │
│   …  (5 more rows)         2.0%       $30,000                             │
│ ▸ META                     0.6%        $9,000     VTI                     │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│   Top 10 shown            18.2%      $273,000                             │
│   + 214 smaller names      4.4%       $66,000                             │
│   Not attributed to a name 77.4%    $1,161,000    ▸ why                    │
│   Total                  100.0%     $1,500,000                            │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Four changes, in priority order:

1. **Residual + total footer.** The tail, the unattributed remainder, and a total
   reading exactly `100.0%`. The "broken math" perception dies here — the table
   closes. *Highest value-per-line change in this spec*, and it needs no new data.
2. **Account provenance in the expansion.** One row per `(wrapper × account)`
   pair. The "and where" half of the ask, and the only part needing a leaf change.
3. **Dollars alongside percents.** "How much do I own" is a dollar question first.
4. **`Where` column names sources, and every row expands.** Today `multi` gates
   both the chevron and the click handler (`:508-513`), so single-source rows are
   inert under rows that aren't.

Closure check: 18.2 + 4.4 + 77.4 = 100.0%; $273,000 + $66,000 + $1,161,000 =
$1,500,000. NVDA's sources sum to its row (3.1 + 3.7 + 0.6 = 7.4%).

**Account-provenance data.** `LookThroughPositionInput` gains `accounts[]` (the
caller already holds it at `portfolio-health.ts:397`), and
`EffectiveNamePosition.sources[]` gains `accountId` / `accountLabel`. Direct
holdings split by their own per-account values; a fund slice splits pro-rata
across the accounts holding *that fund* — **exact, not an estimate** (SPY 60/40
across two accounts means SPY's Apple slice is 60/40). This belongs in the leaf,
not the component: it's pro-rata money math over two joined datasets, and doing it
in the view would duplicate the leaf's fund/not-fund judgment.

## 4. States

| State | Meter | Caption | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| No funds held | 100% | *(none)* | silent |
| 0% attributed | 0% | `$0 of $1.50M sectors seen inside · 5 profiles fetch per visit — reload to improve` | informational |
| Partial (typical) | 37.4% | `$561k of $1.50M sectors seen inside · $423k not attributable · $516k awaiting data` | informational |
| Ceiling reached | 82.0% | `Fully attributed — the remaining $270k can't decompose (policy exclusions)` | **complete, nothing awaited** |

**The ceiling state changes register** — it is reached only when the awaiting-data
group is empty and every remaining opaque fund is a policy exclusion. Then every
"reload" affordance disappears and the language turns terminal.
Implying progress after progress has stopped is how an honest partial-coverage UI
becomes a dishonest one. **No state uses warning colour** (P4).

### 4.1 Making the 0% state renderable

`summarizePortfolioHealth` discards exactly the data the 0% view is made of:

```ts
lookThrough = anyFundAttributed ? "partial" : "none";
if (lookThrough === "none") lookThroughExposure = null;   // portfolio-health.ts:415
```

A book holding 44 funds with none yet attributed receives `null` — no
`sectorResidual`, no `opaqueFunds`. This is the twice-deferred "all-opaque
diagnostics dropped in the `none` state" follow-up (review rounds 4 and 24),
deferred both times because it touches a converged contract. This spec is the
first consumer that needs it.

**Resolution: delete the null-out. Do not add a sibling field.** An earlier draft
proposed a narrow `lookThroughDiagnostics` field (residuals + opaque funds only)
to avoid touching the contract. Two independent arguments kill it:

**1. The sibling discards real data the panel needs.** For a mixed book — direct
positions plus funds that are all opaque — `computeLookThroughExposure` still
computes a genuine `sectorExposure` and `sectorCoveragePct` from the direct
holdings: the `!isFund` branch does `add(sectorMass, bucket, mv)` unconditionally
(`etf-look-through.ts:642`), while `hasAttribution` only flips inside the fund
loops (`:831`, `:934`). So `lookThrough` reads `"none"`, the exposure is nulled,
and **already-computed direct-sector data is thrown away**. A residuals-only
sibling could not render those bars, and the always-on panel could not close its
axis to 100% — which is outcome 1's entire premise.

**2. The consumer audit shows the flip is safe anyway.** There are exactly **two**
runtime consumers, and **both already guard on `lookThrough === "partial"`
independently of the null check**:

| Consumer | Current gate | After the flip |
|---|---|---|
| `health-section.tsx:303` | `lookThrough === "partial" && lookThroughExposure` | relax to `lookThroughExposure != null` — opts in |
| `build-portfolio-context.ts:152` | `lookThrough === "partial" && lookThroughExposure !== null` | unchanged in Phase 2 step 2; ungated with the prompt in step 3 |

Because both re-check `lookThrough` themselves, dropping the null-out is a no-op
for every consumer that doesn't opt in. A third nullable sibling field would add
contract surface to protect an invariant nothing actually relies on.

`lookThroughExposure` becomes: non-null whenever an `etfProfiles` map was
supplied and the leaf produced a read; `lookThrough` remains the *attribution*
verdict. Update the field docblock accordingly. **Test impact:** four assertions
in `portfolio-health.spec.ts` (`:142`, `:366`, `:425`, `:485`) — `:366` is the
no-`etfProfiles` case and is unaffected; the other three need review.

## 5. Visual encoding

Terminal-ish dark UI on OKLCH tokens
([`globals.css`](../../labs/trading-desk/app/globals.css)): 10–11.5px type, mono
figures, `tracking-wider` uppercase titles, 12px (`h-3`) `rounded-sm` bars on
`--c-surface-2` tracks. Keep all of it — conformance beats taste.

| Role | Token |
|---|---|
| Sector bar, meter fill | `--c-accent` — one hue for every bar |
| Residual — not attributable | `--c-fg-faint` |
| Residual — awaiting data | `--c-fg-faint` at ~55% opacity |
| Concentration alert chip | `--c-warn` + `⚠` (unchanged) |
| All text | `--c-fg` / `--c-fg-muted` / `--c-fg-faint` — never the data colour |

Sectors are **nominal categorical** — swapping their order changes nothing — so
every bar takes the same hue and no legend is needed. Don't colour bars by value
or give each sector its own hue: there are more than eight GICS sectors, and
value-colouring double-encodes what bar length already shows.

Two deliberate departures: **the meter never ramps to a warning colour** at low
fill (incompleteness is not severity — P4), and **no texture on the residual**
(reserved for `forced-colors`/print; the label does the work). The residual's two
segments are separated by a **2px gap in the surface colour**, not a border. No
gridlines — every bar is directly labelled.

Before ship, check `--c-fg-faint` at 55% opacity and `--c-warn` against
`--c-surface` at 3:1, both themes, with a contrast validator rather than by eye.

## 6. Deliberately not building

Each was designed in full and cut — defensible, not necessary.

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **Sector → symbol drill-down** (+ a `contributors` leaf field) | §0.2 — only half-answerable: fund-held mass resolves to *"via VTI 4.8%"*, never to symbols. Not worth a leaf field plus a disclosure layer when §3 answers the underlying need completely. **Cheap to revisit:** the direct half already exists in `sectorExposure[].constituents`. |

## 9. Verify

**Before Phase 2:** confirm the unified panel closes to 100% against the live
44-ETF book. Both axes share one denominator and `sectorResidual` already exists,
so once the residual is drawn, **any axis that doesn't close is a real defect** —
in the leaf or the profile map — not a presentation problem.

**Also before Phase 2:** confirm the panel still renders a sector view for a book
holding a priced short, where the leaf returns `null` (Phase 2 step 4). That path
has no look-through data at all, so it is the one case a "renders unconditionally"
panel can silently lose.

**Every derived figure in this spec is machine-checked**, including the coverage
ceiling. Six arithmetic slips have been found and fixed across review rounds. The
fifth (a ceiling stated as 82.0% against a then-correct 60.0%) survived a check
covering only sums and dollar conversions, not *derived* quantities. The sixth was
the 60.0% itself: it subtracted the passing-fund remainder, which the code cannot
show is permanent (§0.6). Any future edit to the worked example must re-run the
full check — sums, dollar conversions, both axes, and the ceiling — not just the
numbers visibly changed.

The ceiling deserves the scrutiny: it is the one figure that tells a user whether
to stop waiting. Overstating it says "nearly complete" over mass that will never
resolve; **understating it tells the user to stop waiting on mass the next refresh
may attribute**, which is the failure this revision fixes. Only mass no refresh
can change may be subtracted.
