# context-supply › it assembles-the-same-brief-every-pass

**Issue:** LAB-136

**Outcome:** One phase of work — review — is handed a brief that is what that phase actually needs, judged once by a human against the written review standard the brief itself carries, and identical byte for byte on every pass. Round eight behaves like round one. The brief is shaped as a standing half (the phase's instructions, the project's grounding, the review standards, the objective) and a small changing half (this issue, this pull request, this diff), and the standing half must survive a change of issue. The human read is a real gate with a real red state: if the brief is missing something a reviewer needs, the verdict is FAIL and the recipe changes. It has already gone red once — see the verdict log.

**Input:** `fixtures/world.json` — a hand-built world with no database and no event feed: two issues, each with a pull request, its diff and what the author verified; the project's grounding; the review standards; the objective they sit under. Identifiers are invented (`ISSUE-1`, `#101`) and never real tracker ids (BP-006). Held-out: `run.mts` reads the world from the fixture and grades every fact against it, so swapping in another valid world still passes a correct recipe. No brief text is hardcoded in the check — the graded set is *derived* by walking the fixture. Swappability is guarded rather than assumed, in the two ways fixture content can break this check's own instruments — both rejected at setup rather than surfacing as a confusing miss. **Overlapping identifiers**: the leak check uses substring containment, so a world whose two issues carry `ISSUE-1` and `ISSUE-10`, or `#10` and `#101`, is rejected instead of reported as a false leak. **Colliding labels**: the region slicing locates its anchors with `indexOf`, so a world whose *content* contains `Issue `, `PR #`, `Verification: ` or a grounding tag in the unescaped prompt is rejected instead of having its regions cut at the wrong offset. Each pass reads a fresh `structuredClone`.

**Signal:**
- **Eight passes, one issue.** Eight assemblies of the recipe, each over a fresh copy of the world, render to the same bytes under one named comparator (`briefsAgree`).
- **The precondition.** Exactly one leading `system` message and none after it — which is what makes "the leading system run *is* the standing half" exact for this recipe rather than a heuristic. If it fails, the boundary derivation is void and the standing-half signal is withdrawn, not reinterpreted.
- **Exactly one model invocation per pass.** Each invocation overwrites the capture, so a second call would silently discard the first handoff and leave every check below grading the *last* brief rather than the one the recipe assembled. This recipe is tool-free and terminal at step 0, so a second call is a regression by definition — asserting one is both the guard and its own canary. Accumulating instead would grade something the design says cannot happen.
- **Every contribution the brief claims to carry is present *and correctly placed*.** The expected set is derived by *walking the fixture world*, never hand-listed — the phase instructions, the project grounding, the review standards and the objective in the standing half; the issue's id, title, body, PR metrics, diff and verification statement in the changing half. A contribution added to the world but never rendered fails here, and the list cannot drift out of agreement with what the brief promises. Placement is graded two ways, because there are two different hazards and **neither mechanism covers the other** — established by test, not by argument:

  - **Across regions.** Each contribution is graded inside *its own region*, not across the whole half. Without this, swapping `project` and `standards`, or `issue.body` and the verification statement, leaves both strings present and every signal green while the model reads each under the other's heading. **The two halves need different mechanisms, and that is honest rather than untidy:** the standing half is tag-structured, so its regions are read off the recipe's own context object — its keys *are* the tags the framework renders, so a recipe that grows a fourth grounding key is covered without anyone updating the check. The changing half is prose with labels and has no tags, so it is sliced at the labels `renderTail` writes (`Issue `, `PR #`, `Verification: `), kept beside the renderer. Forcing the tag mechanism onto the tail would be one mechanism stretched past where it fits. A label that goes missing voids the tail's scoping loudly rather than silently widening the search back to the whole half — which is how this hole appeared in the first place.
  - **Within one region.** Region scoping cannot see a transposition inside a region: all four PR scalars render into the same untagged tail, so `linesAdded` and `linesRemoved` can swap without leaving it. Those fields are graded on their **labelled fragment** — `+47`, `−12`, `3 files`, `#101` — rather than the bare digits. The labels mirror `renderTail` and are kept beside it. (Verified: with the labelled fragments removed and region scoping alone in place, the transposed brief goes **green** again.)

Without all of this the *shape* could stay perfectly stable while the *content* silently vanished or transposed: eight passes would still agree, the split would still hold, the canary would still fire.
- **Two different issues.** The standing half is byte-identical between issue A and issue B; the changing half **differs**; each carries its own contributions; and neither carries the other's identifying facts (id, title, PR number).

**Anti-game:** A hollow pass here looks like one of seven things, and each has a check that closes it. Five of the six are the same failure at different depths — *an instrument that cannot see what it claims to measure, reporting fine* — which is worth naming, because it is the shape every defect found on this issue has taken.
1. *A comparator that could never return false.* → A variant recipe whose grounding appends a **per-call counter** must make the same named `briefsAgree` return false. A counter, never the clock: in-process assemblies can land in one tick, and a flaky check is worse than none. Two assemblies suffice for the canary — a per-call counter differs on the second; the primary loop runs eight because that is the claim's language.
2. *A brief so thin nothing in it could drift.* → Every fact graded is pulled from the fixture; the assembled brief is printed on every run so a human sees what was actually measured.
3. *A stable shape over draining content.* → The derived contribution assertions above. This is the one that makes the goal a regression check rather than a shape check, because the human read that would otherwise catch it is a one-time verdict-log row that `goal:all` never re-runs. (Verified: dropping the diff and the verification statement, then the standards and the objective summary, turns it red and names the missing fixture paths — while every other signal stays green.)
4. *Content present but transposed.* → Labelled-fragment grading for the fields whose bare value is ambiguous. (Verified: swapping `linesAdded` and `linesRemoved` in `renderTail` turns it red naming `+47` and `−12`; before this it was fully green, since both digits were still in the tail.)
5. *A capture that quietly lost the brief it was measuring.* → The one-invocation assertion. (Verified: giving the recipe a tool and scripting a tool call on the first step produces two invocations and turns it red.)
6. *A standing-half comparison between two briefs that are secretly the same brief.* → Requiring the changing halves to differ and to carry their own fixture's facts. Without those two assertions a recipe that assembled issue A twice passes every other signal. (Verified: forcing exactly that turns three assertions red.)
7. *Measuring the wrong mechanism and passing green.* → The model stub is step-capable and its legacy `generate` **throws**. A `generate()`-only stub drives the SDK-owned compatibility path, which contains no `emitGeneratorStep` call sites and which production never takes. (Verified: removing `generateStep` routes the run into `generate()` and the guard fires.)

The stub implements `generateStep` and **not** `streamStep`. Both are optional on `GeneratorModel` (only `modelId` and `generate` are required), and this recipe is tool-free and identity-less, so `canStream` is false and the streaming branch is unreachable — a `streamStep` here would be dead surface. Adding tools or `itemVisibility` later cannot silently reroute to a legacy path either: `canStream` also requires `stream` or `streamStep` to exist, and neither does.

The check must **not** assert on `assembleMessages`'s return value — that is the unit suite's job. This grades what the model was handed, through the step-method path production uses.

**Recipe constraint — load-bearing, not stylistic.** Every dynamic contribution in this recipe must be a **top-level slot function**. No functions may survive inside a slot's returned value.

`resolveSlotValues` calls top-level slot functions and stops; a function nested inside object-form context (`context: { drift: () => … }`) is resolved *later*, by `aggregateContextEntries`, and so never enters the capture. Such a formatter drifts the assembled bytes while the capture reports the slot perfectly stable — and the localization step below would then blame the framework for this recipe's own nondeterminism. That is a false framework finding, which under the issue's decision 3 pauses the epic.

Anyone extending this recipe needs to know that the constraint is what makes attribution sound, not a style preference. It is enforced in the two places where it changes an outcome — inside `localize`, which consults it before it may say "seam", and in the nested-formatter variant, which proves the guard fires. There is deliberately no third, always-on check: on a green run it would restate a constraint nothing is currently relying on.

**Localization — and the correction this issue made to it.** On a red byte comparison, the check attributes the drift before anything is reported: slot outputs are compared **from the same eight runs** (never a fresh re-run, which is a different sample and can report "slots were stable" for drift the slots caused). Slot outputs differ → recipe drift, ours to fix here. Slot outputs agree → the assembly seam, which is a framework finding to file, not to repair here.

Spelled that way alone, the step is unsound. The constraint check has to run **inside** the attribution, first: an unresolved slot means the capture is blind, and blindness is not evidence of stability. Before that ordering was in place, a nested recipe formatter produced a confident *"report and stop — the drift is in the assembly seam"*. It now produces *"ours to fix here, NOT a framework finding."* Both outputs are recorded in the verdict log.

**Coverage this does not have** (stated rather than papered over):
- The **seam** verdict is reachable but has never fired on a real drift. Producing one would require making the framework itself nondeterministic, which means editing `packages/*` — a non-goal of this issue. What is proven is that the classifier reaches that branch only when the capture can see every dynamic contribution.
- A grounding contribution that reads the **clock** is not reliably detected: eight immediate in-process assemblies can share a tick. The honest coverage is per-call variation.
- **Fixture mutation** is not detected either way — every pass starts from a fresh copy, so every pass performs the same mutation on clean data.
- **Grading is substring matching over rendered text, and that is the approach's ceiling** (tracked on FIX-1183, not folded here). `carries` is a bare `includes`, so a body that repeats its own title would let a removed title still be "found". The current fixture has no same-object leaf overlaps, so the recorded verdicts are sound — but every guard in this check (region scoping, escape handling, anchor collision, identifier overlap) exists to protect substring matching against arbitrary substituted content, and the fourth of those was introduced by the third one's fix. Patching the next hole makes another likely, because the approach *is* the hole: substring matching cannot reliably separate *present in the right place* from *present somewhere*. The real fix is structured, field-specific comparison — a redesign of the grading, not another guard.
- **Transposition of two unlabelled fields inside one region** is not detected. Regions are the finest scope the render offers, and within one region only the labelled fragments distinguish fields. `issue.id` carries the `Issue ` label and `linesAdded`/`linesRemoved` carry `+`/`−`, so those are covered; two adjacent unlabelled values in the same region would not be. Closing it further would mean labelling the brief for the test's benefit, which is the tail wagging the dog.
- Variance across **machines, processes, locales, timezones** is not covered. Eight passes seconds apart in one process are materially weaker than "round eight behaves like round one" in production. Do not report a green here as the epic's headline claim having been met.

**Model:** n/a — model-free. The stub records the messages it was handed and returns empty text; it cannot influence the brief, so the assembly under test is the framework's real one. Introducing a real model would put nondeterminism inside a determinism proof and would answer a different question (was the review any good?).

**Run:** `pnpm tsx goals/context-supply/assembles-the-same-brief-every-pass/run.mts`
