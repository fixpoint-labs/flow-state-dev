# coordinators › it hands each post to its delegates

**Issue:** FIX-1791 (epic FIX-1786); leg g, FIX-1826. Its spec's goal: `specs/issues/FIX-1791/SPEC.md` → "The goal, and how we'll know it's met".

**Outcome:** a user's coordinator hands each post to delegates from that user's own roster, by its own judgment, best fit, round robin or everyone. The user, or the coordinator itself, changes the delegates mid-conversation. Each delegate answers each post once per round, answers go back out only within a set number of rounds, every routing decision is recorded, and no other user's worker is ever a delegate.

**Input:**

- Legs a to d, f and g: Shift Manager's DevTeam install as the commit ships it, its chief of staff on the `coordinator` flow, served by Shift Manager's own command over a fresh store. Alice is its owner and Bob its second member, each with their own verified bearer, each through the shipped clients (`createWorkforceClient`, the session client, an action client on the flow the session names). The clients are loaded from the commit under test.
- Leg e: a goal-local tree (`fixtures/workforce/`): three coordinators, `desk.fit` (`best-fit`, fallback `desk.beta`), `desk.turns` (`round-robin`) and `desk.all` (`everyone`, `rounds: 1`), over two delegates, `desk.alpha` and `desk.beta`, on a goal-local `helper` flow. It runs on the real engine and its HTTP router, through the shipped clients, as its own process (`leg-e.mts`). The delegates, the best-fit evaluator and the judgment turn are scripted by marks in the post.
- Held out, picked at run time: every word and slug in an ask, Bob's worker's id, the missing worker's id, which default delegate leg d removes and which roster worker it adds. The asks' wording is in `fixtures/asks.json`; another wording a person might use must pass too.
- Leg a's ask carries its feature line on a line of its own (`{slug}: show a {word} badge …`), the way the EM's door and the docs describe one; b repeats it. Leg a's ask says to hand the work to a delegate, and never which one: the claim is that the chief of staff picks the delegate that does the work, rather than hiring.
- Leg g asks plainly, in a new conversation: "Please get this feature filed for the engineering team:" and the same feature line, never saying to hand it on. Its claim is leg a's, in another wording a person might use. Asked that way, the chief of staff has at times posted the ask to a mailbox, or hired a worker and handed it the work, so the EM never got it (the runs below). Its single attempts pass less often than leg a's, so read its verdict for the attempt it passed on.

**Signal:** each failure is tagged `<leg>:<assertion>`.

- **a** (Alice asks her chief of staff for work her EM delegate does, with a held-out word): `a:no-hire` her own workers don't change; `a:one-delivery` the conversation's ledger holds one delivery of the post, to the EM, delivered; `a:session-120s` and `a:carries-word` the EM's session for this conversation holds the post, with the word, within 120 s; `a:judgment-record` one `coordinator-route` record for the post, `by: judgment`, delivering to the EM; `a:filed` the EM's answer in the conversation says `Filed <id> on the board.`, and the team's board, read as Alice through the mailbox session and the board refs Shift Manager reads, holds that row with the word in its goal.
- **b** (the same ask again): the same four, and `b:same-session` the delivery goes into the session the first ask opened. A first ask that opened none (leg a, run as this leg's setup, red) fails `b:same-session`; it is never skipped.
- **c** ("audit our dependencies' licenses", with the go-ahead to hire if no delegate does it, so a chief of staff that asks first isn't read as one that won't): `c:one-hire` one worker of her own appears; `c:added` it is on this conversation's delegates; `c:delivered` the ask's one delivery goes to it. Asked again: `c:no-second-hire` no new worker; `c:same-worker` a delivery to the same worker.
- **d** (a new conversation): Alice adds one delegate and removes one through the app (`addDelegate`, `removeDelegate`), then asks who its delegates are. `d:read-called` the turn calls `listDelegates`; `d:read-equals-list` its output equals the list the session holds; `d:reply-equals-list` the reply names every worker on that list and no worker off it.
- **e**: `e:evaluated` one evaluator call places a post; `e:held` a follow-up sent while that delegate still works goes to it, `by: held`, with no evaluator call; `e:fallback` a failed call goes to the fallback delegate; `e:fallback-removed` removing that delegate clears the fallback; `e:judgment` then a failed call goes to the coordinator's judgment turn, recorded `by: judgment`; `e:redelivery` the turn hands the post to one delegate twice, and the second is skipped, one delivery and one answer; `e:unplaced` a failed call whose judgment turn fails too is recorded `unplaced` and said in the conversation; `e:round-robin` three posts go alpha, beta, alpha; `e:four-then-none` everyone with `rounds: 1` hands each delegate the post once in round 0 and once in round 1 (the routing records), each of those four deliveries is answered (the ledger), and each delegate's answers in the conversation number exactly two, still two after a grace period: graded per delegate and per round, so three answers from one and one from the other fails.
- **f**: Bob hires a worker of his own. `f:bobs-worker-refused` Alice's `addDelegate` naming it is refused, naming the id, and it is on no list of hers, after the app's add and after the chief of staff's turn; `f:missing-refused` a worker nobody holds is refused by name; `f:same-answer` the two answers are the same but for the id; `f:tool-refused` asked to add Bob's worker, the chief of staff's `addDelegate` tool returns the same refusal (a turn that calls no `addDelegate` shows nothing about the tool, so the ask is made exactly once more, in a fresh conversation; if that second turn calls no `addDelegate` either, `f:tool-refused` FAILs, never NOT RUN or PASS; the list is graded after every turn); `f:create-refused` a session create carrying `delegates` is refused with 400 naming the field, and no session exists at its id.
- **g** (Alice asks plainly, in a new conversation, for work her EM delegate does, with a held-out word): `g:no-hire`, `g:one-delivery`, `g:session-120s`, `g:carries-word` and `g:judgment-record`, as in a. It runs after every other leg, so a worker it hires can't sway one.

**Anti-game:** no delegate is seeded by a fixture or a store write: every change goes through an action, the roster flow's `hire` or a chief-of-staff turn. Every grade reads what that person owns. The conversation's items (its `coordinator-route` records, the turn's tool outputs, the delegates' lines) and the delegate's own session, by the session id the ledger names, are read through the install's routes as that person. The conversation's session record (its `delegates` and its delivery ledger) is read from the store, from the row that person owns: the SQLite file the install writes for legs a to d, f and g, and the engine's session store for leg e. The session route sends a client only the state a flow exposes (FIX-1588), and the coordinator exposes neither field; its ledger carries delivery tokens. Leg d grades the tool's output against the session's list and the reply against both; the list is never read through the delegate read it grades. Leg e counts answers again after a grace period. The engine's request store is read only to wait until nothing runs.

**Attempts:** legs a to d, f and g run on a real model, which doesn't route the same way every time. Each runs until it first passes, up to three attempts (`GOAL_ATTEMPTS`), each on a fresh store and a fresh Lab, and each running only the legs still red. A leg's verdict names the attempt it passed on, and every attempt is printed, so the verdict log records how often a leg passes, not only that it did. Under a control there is one attempt. Leg e is scripted and runs once.

**Model:** real — `anthropic/claude-haiku-5-5` (the DevTeam chief of staff's own setting) on legs a to d, f and g; leg e is scripted and needs none. The run reads the model from that file and needs a key that serves it here (`keysServing` in `goals/lib/model.mts`): for an Anthropic model, `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY`, since this repo installs no direct Anthropic or OpenRouter provider. Without one, legs a to d, f and g are blocked and say so.

**Model-free run:** `GOAL_LEGS=e`

**Run:** `pnpm tsx goals/coordinators/hands-each-post-to-its-delegates/run.mts`. `GOAL_LEGS=a,b,…` runs some legs; `GOAL_COMMIT=<sha>` serves another commit from its own tree and install (`git archive` plus `pnpm install`, kept under the system temp directory across runs). On demand only, never a CI gate.

**Controls:** each is one scratch patch to one Workforce module, applied as the process under test loads it (`goals/lib/module-patch.mjs` through `NODE_OPTIONS`; printed in full by the run, and refused when it never reached the code). A control runs the one leg it targets.

- `GOAL_CONTROL=no-roster-check`: the delegate check takes a worker that isn't on the user's roster as if it were (`coordinator-check.ts`). Leg f must fail **`f:bobs-worker-refused`** (*Bob's worker is a delegate*). `f:missing-refused` and `f:tool-refused` go red with it: the same check answers all three, so none has a red state of its own. `f:create-refused` stays green: the create is refused before any check runs.
- `GOAL_CONTROL=no-round-limit`: a coordinator whose `rounds:` is above 0 runs to the ceiling of 3 (`coordinator-flow.ts`, the worker's config read). Leg e must fail **`e:four-then-none`**, and nothing else: the patch leaves `rounds: 0` alone.
- `GOAL_CONTROL=no-delegate-read`: the delegate read answers from the worker's defaults, not the conversation's list (`coordinator-flow.ts`). Leg d must fail **`d:reply-equals-list`** (*the reply equals the list*), with `d:read-equals-list`.

**Before-state:** `GOAL_COMMIT=bfe68d89e` is `main` just before FIX-1791's first PR merged (#2865). Every leg must fail there.
