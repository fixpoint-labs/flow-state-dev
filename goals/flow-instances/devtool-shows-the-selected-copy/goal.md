# flow-instances › the DevTool shows the copy you selected

**Issue:** FIX-1324 (DevTool instance list/switch/sessions), on the FIX-1321/1322 identity cutover and FIX-1323 isolation
**Outcome:** A developer running two copies of one flow definition on one server can tell them apart in the DevTool and trust what they are looking at. Each copy is a row of its own, identified by its exact id. Selecting one selects its sessions and its requests; switching to the other shows the other's work and none of the first's, including in the frame the switch produces. The controls on screen act on the copy that is selected. A single-copy app's experience is unchanged.
**Input:** `fixtures/input.json` — two collection instance ids with the marker each writes, plus a singleton. Held-out: the ids and markers are read from the fixture and graded against what appears on screen and on the wire, so swapping them must still pass a correct implementation.
**Signal:** the shipped DevTool bundle served by the real `fsdev dev`, over HTTP and SSE, driven in Chromium. (a) Both copies appear as separate rows carrying their exact ids, distinguishable by ACCESSIBLE NAME and not only by styling, beside the singleton's row. (b) Creating a session under copy A and dispatching its action shows A's marker in the stream and never B's; the dispatch goes to `/api/flows/<A>/…`. (c) Clicking B's row directly — the switch an operator makes — leaves no marker of A's on screen in the frame the click produces, AND issues no request pairing B's address with a request id of A's. (d) B gets a different session, its own request and its own marker, addressed to `/api/flows/<B>/…`. (e) Returning to A restores A's own session and its work alone. (f) Each session's client state holds its own copy's marker. (g) The singleton runs on its unchanged address, its session list is read on `flowKind` while a collection member's is read on `flowId`. (h) After a full page reload, each copy is offered back its own saved session and never its peer's.
**Anti-game:** a hollow pass would assert that requests complete, that two rows render, or that the right text eventually appears. All three hold while the panel is still addressing the wrong copy. So the check MUST grade the marker each handler writes (not the request status), MUST read the switching frame with nothing awaited between the click and the observation (the defect IS a frame — an earlier build passed every settled-state check while issuing `GET /api/flows/engineer-b/requests/<A's request>/stream` on the way through), MUST grade the actual request URLs rather than the rendered text alone, and MUST reload the page so the persisted selection is exercised rather than in-memory state. Grading `flowKind` proves nothing: both copies share it.
**Model:** n/a — handlers only; the property under test is identity and retirement, not model output.
**Run:** `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers pnpm tsx goals/flow-instances/devtool-shows-the-selected-copy/run.mts`

> **Why this one needs a browser.** Every part of the outcome lives in the composition rather than in any one hook: the flash is a commit, the address is assembled from the selection at the moment a control fires, and the retirement is a race between an effect and a render. A component suite can pin each piece and still let the assembled panel show one copy's work under another — which is exactly what happened here on the first run.
