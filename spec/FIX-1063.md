# FIX-1063: Unavailable fundamentals/indicators/macro payloads zero-fill instead of null — Implementation Spec

## Part I — The Case *(for the human)*

### 1. Problem — why it matters, why now

When the desk cannot get a number, it currently writes down zero and keeps going.

A company whose market cap the data provider didn't return is recorded as a company worth
**$0**. A stock with three months of trading history — too short to compute a 200-day
average — is recorded as having a 200-day average of **zero**, and is then labelled a
**flat trend** the desk never measured. A day when the government data feed answers for six
of its nine series is recorded as **0% inflation, 0% unemployment, 0% interest rates** for
the three it missed.

These are not display glitches. A missing market cap enters the valuation arithmetic as a
real zero: price-to-book comes out as zero, and enterprise value comes out equal to the
company's net debt with the entire equity value silently dropped. **Every enterprise-value
multiple then reads as radically cheap on data nobody has** — the exact input pattern behind
a "this looks like a bargain" call on a name we know nothing about.

**Why now.** Two things changed. The desk now takes real positions against these numbers,
and the failure is silent — nothing in a finished report distinguishes a measured zero from
a missed read, so nobody has ever caught one. And the epic this issue sits under
([FIX-1067](https://linear.app/fixpoint-labs/issue/FIX-1067)) has four other issues queued
behind this one: they each build on top of the same numbers, so building them first means
building them twice.

There is no workaround. A reader cannot tell from a finished report which zeros were real.

### 2. Solution in plain terms

**A figure the desk did not observe is recorded as "unavailable" rather than as zero** —
in the raw data, in the arithmetic built on it, in what the analysts read, and in the
report. Nothing downstream is allowed to quietly turn an unavailable back into a zero to
keep a ratio or a chart drawing.

The rule is decided by **where a number came from**, not by which file produced it. Three
kinds of producer break it today and all three are fixed: the builders that fire when a
provider is completely unreachable; the technical math that returns zero when a stock is
too young to compute an indicator; and the two fundamentals adapters, which fill in zeros
for anything a provider left out of an otherwise-successful answer. That last one is the
most common and the most dangerous — nothing marks the value as missing, because the fetch
*worked*.

Reports **already generated** cannot be repaired. An old record carries nothing that
separates a missing zero from a genuinely measured one, so recomputing it would mean
guessing which zeros were real — the same dishonesty one layer down. So new reports carry a
stamp saying they were produced under the honest rule, and a report without that stamp is
flagged as pre-fix. The guarantee is forward-looking, and the report says so on its face.

**Philosophy** — tenet 1 (coherence): the desk already treats an unobserved *statement*
figure as null; this makes the rest of the data agree with it rather than carrying two
opposite conventions. Tenet 5 (one convergence point): the legacy-zero handling lands at two
named boundaries rather than as a scattering of `if (x === 0)` checks that the next reader
has to find.

> **§2 then §6 lead, and the rest is derivation.**

### 6. Decisions & rules — the sign-off surface

1. **What counts as a fabricated zero is decided by where the number came from, not by
   which three fields the issue named.**
   Every producer that reports a figure it did not observe is fixed: the five empty-payload
   builders (fundamentals, indicators, macro, social sentiment, reddit mentions), the
   short-history technical math, the partly-answered macro read, and both live fundamentals
   adapters.
   *Rejected:* fixing only the three payload builders in the issue title — the remaining ones
   would keep fabricating, and the next person to find one would have to reopen this work.
   *Locks in:* every future data source owes the same discipline. A new one that fills in a
   figure it didn't measure is a defect, not a style choice, and we will be held to that.

2. **A trend we could not measure is reported as no trend, not as "flat".**
   *Rejected:* nulling the numbers underneath and leaving the "flat" label — that produces a
   report asserting a finding it never made.
   *Locks in:* some reports get visibly less confident. A recently-listed company's report
   will say the trend could not be read rather than showing a definite-looking answer, and
   that will look like a regression to anyone who liked the old certainty.

3. **A fundamentals read with no market cap counts as thin evidence, so the desk refuses to
   add to a position on it.**
   *Rejected:* letting the valuation math's own nulls carry it indirectly — they mostly
   would, but "mostly" is not a real-money gate.
   *Locks in:* more runs land on "don't add". That is the correct direction to be wrong in
   for real money, and it will occasionally hold back a setup that was genuinely fine.
   Reversing it later is cheap; the runs it declined in the meantime are not recoverable.

4. **Reports generated before this lands are marked, not repaired.**
   New reports carry a stamp; a report without one reads as pre-fix.
   *Rejected:* recomputing old reports — nothing in them says which zeros were real, so any
   recompute is a guess wearing a decimal point.
   *Locks in:* we can never claim an old report's numbers are honest, and no later work in
   this set may promise more than a forward-looking guarantee.

5. **A stored zero is converted to "unavailable" only where the zero is provably a fill** —
   a payload the desk itself tagged as unavailable at the time. A zero recorded under a live
   provider tag is left exactly as it is.
   *Rejected:* converting every stored zero — on a live-tagged record a zero may be a real
   measurement (a company that genuinely pays no dividend), and flipping it would invent a
   gap where there was a fact.
   *Locks in:* a small number of old recorded data snapshots keep their ambiguous zeros
   until someone re-records them. This is a development-corpus limitation, not a
   user-visible one.

6. **This issue ships the pre-fix *flag*; the visible "generated before the data-honesty
   fix" notice on the report is [FIX-1060](https://linear.app/fixpoint-labs/issue/FIX-1060)'s.**
   *Rejected:* shipping a notice here too — it would collide with FIX-1060 inside the same
   component, and the epic already split this seam.
   *Locks in:* between this landing and FIX-1060 landing, an old report carries the flag but
   shows nothing to the reader. That window is real and is the price of not serialising the
   two issues.

7. **Three PRs, because the type change cascades but the other two halves don't depend on
   each other.**
   *Rejected:* one PR (a ~20-file diff nobody can review) and five PRs (four of which
   wouldn't compile alone).
   *Locks in:* the contract PR and the marking PR can be reviewed in parallel; the legacy
   handling waits on the contract.

**Non-goals** — repairing or migrating already-stored reports (decision 4); the reader-facing
notice on the Summary (decision 6, FIX-1060's); `composite-math.ts`, which zero-weights a
missing term but returns `missingInputs` alongside the score, so it already labels rather
than fabricates and is the epic's stated exclusion; and any new data provider or paid feed
(the epic's standing fence).

**Size:** Large — ~20 production files · 14 test behaviours · 1 doc surface (`labs/trading-desk/CLAUDE.md`; no `apps/docs` change — see §11) · 3 PRs.

### 3. Tradeoffs & alternatives

**Alternative: leave the values and fix the labels.** Keep the zeros, and have every consumer
check the provenance tag before reading a number. Rejected for two reasons. The tag is only
honest for the *completely unreachable* case — the sparse-live case reports a live source, so
there is no tag to check. And it puts the burden on every current and future reader, which is
how the zero survives in the one path nobody updated.

**The simpler approach: null the three empty-payload builders and stop.** This is literally
what the issue title asks for, and it is a two-file change. Insufficient because it fixes the
*rarest* path. A provider being totally unreachable is uncommon; a provider answering with
some fields missing is routine — and that path is strictly worse, because the payload carries
a live source tag, so nothing marks the value as missing at all. Fixing only the empty-payload
builders would let this issue close while new reports keep fabricating.

**Where the complexity is:** not the nulling. It is the **already-stored** data. Three
separate stores hold the old shape — recorded data snapshots, the per-run valuation record,
and finished reports — and only the first two can be normalised at read time. The third
cannot be repaired at all, which is why §6 decision 4 exists and why a third of this work is
about marking rather than fixing.

**A second, quieter cost:** widening these figures to "possibly unavailable" makes the type
checker walk every piece of arithmetic that consumes them. Most of that arithmetic already
handles unavailable inputs correctly (the valuation module was written that way); the ones
that don't are exactly the bugs this issue is about. The cascade is the feature, but it does
mean the first PR touches a lot of files for a small semantic change.

### 4. Focus practices (1–5)

1. **BP-030 (tolerate the old shape)** — three stores carry the zero-filled shape. An old
   record's `0` must not be re-read as a *new* honest zero, and a new record's `null` must
   not crash a legacy read path. This is the single most likely way to ship a regression here.
2. **BP-020 (live mode never falls back to fixtures)**, in its honesty sense — the reason
   this issue exists. An unobserved figure is not a data-quality nicety; it is the real-money
   gate.
3. **One convergence point for the legacy handling** (tenet 5) — one shared normaliser at two
   named boundaries, not twelve consumers each writing their own zero check. A downstream
   issue that finds itself writing one has hit a gap here; it comments up rather than
   building a second.
4. **BP-035 (second-path checklist)** — the second path here is the *partly successful* fetch,
   not the failed one. A test suite that only covers "provider down" proves nothing about the
   path this issue is really about.

### 5. What using it looks like

The public surface an app developer writes against does not change — this is internal to the
trading-desk lab app. What changes is what the desk's own data carries, so the examples below
are the payloads and the reader-visible result rather than call sites.

**A fundamentals read where the provider omitted market cap** *(what this spec proposes):*

```
before  { source: "finnhub", marketCap: 0,    priceToSales: 0,    returnOnEquity: 0.34 }
after   { source: "finnhub", marketCap: null, priceToSales: null, returnOnEquity: 0.34 }
```

**What the valuation arithmetic then reports:**

```
before  Enterprise value: $12.4B (= net debt; the equity value silently dropped)
        Price/Book: 0.00× (cheap)
        EV/Sales: 0.31× (cheap)
after   Enterprise value: n/a
        Price/Book: n/a
        EV/Sales: n/a
```

**A stock with three months of trading history:**

```
before  { source: "yahoo", sma200: 0, rsi14: 0, trend: "flat" }
after   { source: "yahoo", sma200: null, rsi14: null, trend: null }
```

**Opening a report generated before this landed** *(the flag; the notice is FIX-1060's):*

```
the stored report carries no honesty-contract stamp
  → the report data is flagged as generated before the data-honesty fix
  → its stored zeros are never re-presented as current measurements
```

## Part II — The Build Plan *(for the implementing agent)*

### 7. Technical design

Three layers, in the order a number travels: **produce it honestly**, **tolerate the old
shape where we read it back**, and **mark what we cannot fix**.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  subgraph P[1 · produce]
    EP[empty-payload builders<br/>provider unreachable]
    IM[indicator math<br/>short history]
    FR[macro read<br/>partial FRED]
    AD[fundamentals adapters<br/>sparse response]
  end
  subgraph S[schemas]
    TS[tool output schemas<br/>number → number \| null]
  end
  subgraph C[2 · consume]
    VAL[valuation + setup score<br/>+ evidence gate]
    PR[analyst prompts<br/>raw payload JSON]
  end
  subgraph L[3 · tolerate + mark]
    NORM[shared legacy normaliser]
    STAMP[version stamp<br/>on the run]
  end
  EP & IM & FR & AD --> TS --> VAL & PR
  NORM -->|fixture load| TS
  NORM -->|spine ingest| VAL
  STAMP -->|read at the persisted-report boundary| RPT[report data<br/>carries a pre-fix flag]
```

- **`tools/schemas.ts`** — the three payload schemas (fundamentals, indicators, macro) plus
  the two sentiment ones widen from `z.number()` to nullable. These are **handler** output
  schemas, not generator outputs, so BP-016's strict rules do not bind them (they are
  deliberately absent from the strict walker in `output-schemas-strict.spec.ts` — keep them
  out). The `trend` enum widens to nullable rather than gaining a fourth member: "we did not
  measure it" is an absence, not a fourth trend.
- **`tools/empty-payloads.ts`** — five builders emit `null`, matching the three statement
  builders that already do. Update the module header, which currently advertises the zeros
  as a feature (BP-034 — provenance).
- **`tools/indicators-math.ts`** — every short-history short-circuit returns `null` instead
  of `0`, and `trendLabel` returns `null` when either moving average is unavailable. Its
  defensive non-finite coercion also becomes `null` rather than `0`, on the same reasoning: a
  value the math could not produce is unobserved, not zero. The `IndicatorOutput` type widens
  accordingly.
- **`tools/data/get_macro_indicators.ts`** — the eight `?? 0` defaults become `null`, and
  the CPI year-over-year calculation returns `null` when the year-ago print is absent rather
  than `0`. The `source: "fred"` tag on a partial answer stays as-is — the epic fixes that
  *the values must not imply otherwise*; whether a partial payload should report partial
  provenance is a separate question and is **not** taken here (§12).
- **`lib/providers/finnhub.ts` / `lib/providers/yahoo.ts`** — the fundamentals adapters stop
  defaulting. Finnhub's `pct()` helper and Yahoo's `numberFrom()` are the two convergence
  points; both already have nullable siblings (`nullablePct`, `nullableNumberFrom`) written
  for the P/E work, so this is switching the fundamentals fields onto the existing honest
  helper rather than inventing one. **Do not change `numberFrom`'s other callers** — the
  company-profile path reads it defensively on purpose.
- **The deterministic consumers** — `lib/valuation.ts`, `expected-return.ts`, `fair-value.ts`,
  `dcf.ts`, `setup-score.ts`, `valuation-spine.ts`, and `tools/data/get_factor_ranks.ts`.
  Most already handle unavailable inputs (`valuation.ts`'s `sum()` and `ratio()` return null
  when any input is null, which is exactly right); the compiler will find the ones that
  don't. Two need real thought rather than a type fix, and they are called out in §8.
- **`lib/evidence-gate.ts`** — `deriveCriticalDataThin` gains the market-cap check
  (decision 3).
- **New: a shared legacy normaliser** under `tools/runtime/`. One exported function, applied
  at the two ingest boundaries (§8 step 6). It is a **no-op on post-fix data** by
  construction, so it costs nothing once the corpus turns over.
- **`state.ts` + `orchestration/guards.ts`** — the run carries a nullable honesty-contract
  version, stamped at `seedSession`. Session state is the right home rather than the
  valuation record: it is written on *every* run including one that stops before the
  valuation is computed, it already reaches both persisted-report read boundaries, and the
  nullable-with-null-default convention there is the established BP-030 shape (the
  `householdTickerWeightPct` / `riskMandate` precedent). Absent → pre-fix.
- **`components/summary/report-summary.tsx` + `aggregate.ts`** — the component reads the
  stamp off session state alongside the stop fields it already reads, and passes it into the
  report view model, which grows one boolean. **This is the only edit this issue makes inside
  `components/summary/`** — FIX-1060 owns the rest of that directory, and the epic gives this
  issue the marker seam only. Land it before FIX-1060 if both are in flight.
- **`run-artifacts.ts`** — the exported bundle carries the same flag, from the same session
  field it already reads five other fields off.
- **Untouched:** `composite-math.ts` (the epic's stated exclusion — it returns `missingInputs`
  alongside its score, so it labels rather than fabricates), every generator output schema,
  the statement tools (already nullable), and the price-history path.

**No solution sketch, and no POC.** The change extends a pattern that already ships in this
exact codebase — the three statement payloads were moved from zero-fill to nullable by
FIX-714, and the P/E fields by FIX-692, with the nullable helper functions still sitting in
the two adapter files. Pointing at that is better than re-sketching it. Nothing here rests on
an unverified premise: the two claims that would have earned a POC (that a missing market cap
corrupts enterprise value, and that the macro read stamps a live source on a partial answer)
are both readable directly in `lib/valuation.ts` and `get_macro_indicators.ts`, and the latter
is recorded in that module's own header as having happened.

### 8. Implementation sequence

**PR a — the nullability contract.** Steps 1–5 are one PR because widening a schema and
fixing its consumers do not compile apart.

1. **Widen the five tool output schemas** in `tools/schemas.ts`, including the `trend` enum.
   Nothing else changes yet; the build breaks, and the break list is the work.
   *Test:* each schema accepts null for each widened field and still rejects a wrong type.
   *Depends on:* nothing.
2. **Null the five empty-payload builders.** *Test:* an unavailable payload for each of the
   five tools carries null, not zero, in every unobserved numeric field.
   *Depends on:* 1.
3. **Null the three live producers** — short-history indicator math (including the trend
   label), the partial-FRED macro read, and the two fundamentals adapters.
   *Test:* the §10 producer behaviours. *Depends on:* 1.
4. **Fix the deterministic consumers.** Mechanical for most; two are not:
   - **`setup-score.ts` momentum.** Today a present-but-empty indicator payload scores
     `50 − 10 = 40` — it reads two null moving averages as a *death cross* and counts the
     component toward the evidence threshold. It must contribute nothing when the trend and
     both averages are unavailable, and must not increment the component count.
   - **`get_factor_ranks.ts`.** It already carries the per-consumer `!== 0` coercion the epic
     warns about; replace it with a null check rather than leaving both.
   *Test:* the §10 consumer behaviours. *Depends on:* 1–3.
5. **Widen the evidence gate** (decision 3). *Test:* a live-tagged fundamentals payload with
   no market cap gates new exposure. *Depends on:* 4.

**PR b — legacy tolerance** *(depends on PR a).*

6. **Build the shared normaliser and apply it at the two ingest boundaries** — the fixture
   loader (`tools/runtime/fixtures.ts`) and the valuation-spine ingest (`compute-spine.ts`).
   Per decision 5 it converts zeros to null **only** on a payload tagged `unavailable`, where
   the empty-payload builder is provably the only producer and every zero is therefore
   provably a fill. A live-tagged zero is left alone. *Test:* the §10 legacy behaviours.
   *Depends on:* PR a.
7. **Remove** the module-header claims that describe the zeros as intentional
   (`empty-payloads.ts`'s "The analyst sees explicit zeros", `indicators-math.ts`'s "returns
   `0` … rather than throwing") and the `labs/trading-desk/CLAUDE.md` line that scopes the
   nullable discipline to the statement tools. Leaving them is how the next reader
   re-introduces the zero. *Depends on:* 6.

**PR c — the version stamp and the pre-fix flag** *(independent of a and b; land it early, per
decision 6 and the epic's adjacency note).*

8. **Add the nullable honesty-contract version to session state and stamp it at
   `seedSession`.** *Test:* a new run carries it; a session persisted without it parses and
   reads as pre-fix. *Depends on:* nothing.
9. **Read it at the two persisted-report boundaries** — the Summary component (threading one
   boolean onto the report view model) and the run-artifacts bundle. *Test:* the §10 marking
   behaviours. *Depends on:* 8.

**PR plan.**

|---|---|---|
| a | the nullability contract — schemas, builders, live producers, deterministic consumers, evidence gate (steps 1–5) | — |
| b | the shared legacy normaliser at the fixture-load and spine-ingest boundaries, plus the provenance cleanup (steps 6–7) | a |
| c | the version stamp on new runs and the pre-fix flag at the persisted-report boundary (steps 8–9) | — |

### 9. Edge cases & error handling

| Case | Expected |
|---|---|
| A genuinely measured zero — a company with zero debt, a real 0% dividend yield | Stays `0`. The rule is *unobserved* → null, not *falsy* → null. This is the single most likely way to over-apply the change. |
| A negative real value — a loss-making company's ROE, a negative yield-curve slope | Untouched. Nothing here keys on sign. |
| The macro read answers for some series | Partial payload, nulls for the misses, `source: "fred"` unchanged (§7 — the provenance question is deliberately not taken here) |
| The live social-sentiment route (a generator, not a handler) | Its **generator** output schema stays non-nullable and strict — the model measured a score, so `0` there is a real reading. Only the tool's output schema widens. Do not add it to the strict walker's exceptions. |
| An old recorded data snapshot tagged with a live provider, containing a zero | Left alone (decision 5). Remedy is re-recording, not coercion. |
| An old recorded snapshot tagged `unavailable`, containing zeros | Normalised to null at load — provably a fill. |
| A session persisted before the version stamp existed | Parses (nullable, defaults null) and reads as pre-fix. |
| A run that stops before the valuation is computed | Carries the stamp anyway (it is set at seed), and has no fabricated valuation to mark. |
| A report opened between this landing and FIX-1060 landing | Flag present, no reader-visible notice (decision 6). Not a defect. |
| Analyst prompts | They read the raw payload as JSON, so `null` renders as `null` — self-describing, no formatter change. Verify the technical and macro prompts don't instruct the model to read a zero as a reading. |

**Taxonomy:** nothing here introduces a new error path. Every case degrades to "unavailable",
which the desk's prompts, formatters, and gates already handle — that is the whole point of
matching the existing statement-tool convention rather than inventing one.

### 10. Testing strategy

**Goal check:** the existing `goals/trading-desk-headless/fixture-run-clean` covers the happy
path and must keep passing. This issue's own goal is narrower and does not need a real model:
run the analyse pipeline against a ticker whose fundamentals payload is unavailable and assert
via the zero-model `runSummary` read that the run produced a decision **and** that its
evidence verdict is `insufficient-evidence` — i.e. the desk declined to add on data it does
not have. The `verify-trading-desk` skill is the harness; no new one.

**CI specs** (behaviours, not test names):

- **One behaviour per producer** — for each of the five empty-payload builders, the
  short-history indicator math, the partial-macro read, and each of the two fundamentals
  adapters: an unobserved field reads unavailable, not zero.
- **The second path (BP-035), stated separately because it is the one a naive suite misses:**
  a *successful* provider response with fields missing. This is the sparse-live case, and it
  is the most common path in the whole contract.
- **What must not change:** a genuinely measured zero survives as zero, through the producer,
  the schema, and the arithmetic. Assert this explicitly — it is the over-application failure.
- **The arithmetic behaviours from §5:** with no market cap, enterprise value, price-to-book
  and the EV multiples all report unavailable rather than a number. Assert on the *values*,
  not on the formatted strings.
- **The momentum trap:** a present-but-unavailable indicator payload contributes no momentum
  component and does not count toward the evidence threshold. This is the one consumer where
  the naive type fix leaves a live bug.
- **The evidence gate (decision 3):** a live-tagged fundamentals payload with no market cap
  gates new exposure.
- **The trend label (decision 2):** unavailable moving averages produce no trend, and no
  consumer reads the absence as "flat".
- **Legacy tolerance (decision 5), both directions:** a stored `unavailable`-tagged payload
  with zeros normalises to null on load; a stored live-tagged payload with zeros does not.
- **The marking behaviours (decision 4):** a session persisted without the stamp parses and
  reads as pre-fix; a new run reads as post-fix; the flag reaches both persisted-report read
  boundaries.

**Discipline:** `tdd`. Every seam above is reachable from a vitest spec — the producers are
pure functions or handlers testable with the mock context, the arithmetic is pure, and the
existing suite already has the fixtures (`valuation.spec.ts`, `indicators-math.spec.ts`,
`get-macro-indicators.spec.ts`, `finnhub-fundamentals.spec.ts`, `evidence-gate.spec.ts`,
`setup-score` coverage in `valuation-spine.spec.ts`). Extend those rather than adding a
parallel suite.

**Open questions:** none. Two things a reader might expect here are deliberately settled

issue or two (**one**: the marking half is nine files and shares nothing with any other
issue, and splitting it would leave the contract landing with no way to mark what it can't
fix).

**Follow-ups (flagged, not built):**

- **Partial provenance.** If a partly-answered payload later needs to *say* it was partial
  rather than only carrying nulls, that is a change to the provenance tag vocabulary across
  every tool, and it belongs in the data-and-providers lane, not here.
- **Deepening:** `tools/empty-payloads.ts` is a 380-line hand-maintained table with one
  builder per tool and no compile-time guarantee that a builder's unobserved fields are
  nullable. Out of scope; follow up via `improve-codebase-architecture`.
- **Re-recording the data corpus.** Decision 5 leaves live-tagged zeros in old recorded
  snapshots. A re-record pass would clear them; it is a development-corpus chore, not part
  of this change.

## Spec evolution

- **Spec drafted** — framed as fabrication rather than labelling, because a missing market
  cap corrupts the valuation arithmetic rather than merely displaying badly. Defined the
  contract by producer provenance rather than by the three fields the issue title names, so
  the sparse-live path (the most common one, and the one carrying a live source tag) is
  covered by construction. Took marking over migration for already-stored reports, on the
  epic's forced call. Split into three PRs: the type cascade forces the contract into one
  diff, but the legacy handling and the version stamp are separable and the stamp is
  independent.
